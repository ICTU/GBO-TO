    Findings¶

    A finding records what the demo build showed about the design. A finding is one of these four types:

    • An implicit requirement.
    • An assumption that the build made visible.
    • A gap.
    • An error in a source.

    Each finding records four items:

    • Its trigger: a demo increment or a demo PR.
    • The observation.
    • Why the observation matters for the GO, the PSA, or the FR-DvTP.
    • Where it landed: a requirement, an architecture decision, a risk, or an open question.

    A finding has one of these status values:

    • open: the design has no answer to the finding.
    • closed: the design or a review answered the finding.
    • resolved in demo: the demo changed, and the finding no longer applies.
    • superseded.
    • premise lapsed.

    Finding numbers are never reused. F-01, F-05, F-10, F-12, F-13, F-33, and F-48 do not exist.

    For the open findings per PSA theme, see Summary for the PSA.

    F-19: The register does not record the consent-use event ("first used"), so the FR-22 usage status cannot be shown¶

    • Status: open

    The consent detail page shows when the citizen gave a consent, when a service provider (dienstverlener) first used it, and when it ended. The consent register (toestemmingsregister) keeps only the start and end dates, so the demo cannot show the first use. FR-11 and FR-22 require that the register itself records whether and when data was first retrieved. The open point is how the register learns of a successful delivery at the source. Three options exist: the first status lookup by a PDP, a report from the source after delivery, or derivation from the LDV chain.

    Evidence, impact and proposal
    • Type: capability gap
    • Trigger: SL-06b. The consent detail page shows a history with issued, first used, and revoked or expired.
    • Observation: the register keeps created_at and valid_until, but no use event (consent-register/main.go#L36-L46). The decision logs hold consent_id per allow. These logs are a query log and hold no consent state. The demo shows only issued and revoked or expired.
    • Why it matters: FR-11 v0.9 and FR-22 v0.9 require that the consent register records whether and when data was first retrieved. So the first use is a field in the register. The register does not derive it from the audit log. Single use no longer applies (Q-04), so this finding does not depend on Q-04 any more.
      • (A) The first status query by a PDP.
      • (B) A report from the source after delivery. Option B adds an interface per source.
      • (C) Derivation from the LDV chain. Option C depends on Q-08 and Q-25.

      Proposal: the open point is how the register learns of a successful delivery at the source. Q-42 gives three options:

      The build choice waits in demo issue #389.

    • Related: REQ-76, REQ-14, Q-42, Q-25, Q-08, F-97 (FR-22 to FR-24 name the statuses differently).

    F-47: Wallet credential where the subject of the claim differs from the discloser¶

    • Status: open

    In the death certificate case, a surviving partner discloses her own PID and gets a credential about her deceased partner. The source decides which marriage and partner apply, and the PDP sees only the output fields, so policy cannot check the relation. The PSA assumes that a citizen gets credentials about himself or herself only. The proposal is that the source publishes the relation as filter arguments in its schema, and policy requires fixed values for them. What a filter cannot express stays a tested property of the source, marked in its service description.

    Evidence, impact and proposal
    • Type: implicit requirement / architectural variant
    • Trigger: SL-43 (death certificate, PR #125). A surviving partner discloses her own PID and gets a credential about her deceased partner.
    • Observation: the query is a source-owned view, akteVanOverlijden(bsn). The source picks the marriage and the deceased partner (brp-graphql-server/main.go#L671-L679). The PDP sees only the flat output fields. It cannot inspect how the source walks the relation (eud0002.rego#L7-L11).
    • Why it matters: the PSA assumes self-disclosure (PSA S11). Issuance about a related person changes who can receive a credential about whom. If only the source knows the relation, policy cannot check it.
    • Proposal: the rewritten REQ-57 takes this approach:
      • The preferred option: the source publishes the relation as filter arguments in its schema.
      • Policy then requires the path and the filters with fixed values, without looking at the content. The iWlz network model works this way.
      • What a filter cannot express stays a tested invariant of the source. The service description marks it. Demo issue #425 holds the test (not built).
      • Authorized representatives are out of scope. A mandate is representation, not a registration fact (PSA B-19).
    • Related: REQ-57, REQ-116, AV-10, AV-11, Q-14, Q-20, F-57.

    F-51: The PDP protects its own management endpoints with the decision policy¶

    • Status: open

    The PDP protects its own management endpoints with the policy that decides on requests for citizen data. That policy refuses everything except GBO data requests, so a push of policy bundles from the PAP fails. The demo therefore lets the PDP pull its bundles at start-up, which meets the PSA. The weak point is in the implementation: every rule that allows management sits in the policy for citizen data. The proposal is a separate authorization path for management, as a guideline for bronhouders, because each source keeps its own PAP and PDP.

    Evidence, impact and proposal
    • Type: exposed design choice (management plane versus decision plane)
    • Trigger: SL-44 tried to push bundles from the PAP to the PDP.
    • Observation:
      • The PDP authorizes its own management endpoints with the policy set that decides data requests (package authz).
      • That policy refuses anything that is not a GBO data request, so a push gets 403.
      • For push to work, the decision policy needs a rule that opens the management API of the PDP. The demo therefore pulls at start-up (targets: [], gbo-pdp.yaml#L18-L32).
      • While its policy store is empty, the engine also installs a no-auth mode.
      • On the Manager side, the bundle pull needs its own Cedar policy since OpenFTV v0.1.0 (pdp-bundle.cedar).
    • Why it matters: every rule that allows management sits in the policy that decides on citizen data. Pull meets PSA S06, which asks only that a component reliably gets a valid policy version. The GO and the PSA have no gap here. The gap is in the implementation.
    • Proposal: a PDP needs a separate authorization path for management. Then push works without a wider decision policy (RL-02). The point was reported to the OpenFTV maintainers. Q-21 keeps PAP and PDP at each source. RL-02 therefore stays a guideline for bronhouders and is no requirement.
    • Related: RL-02, F-09, F-52, Q-21, ADR-0021.

    F-52: The policy store is cumulative and cannot delete, and one duplicate package breaks every decision¶

    • Status: open

    The PDP compiles all its policies as one set. In the demo, one stale policy with a duplicate package name made every request fail with a server error instead of a deny. The policy store also cannot delete a policy, so an operator can only retire it. Faults of the same class came back three times, for example a policy that did not compile but showed as loaded. The proposal is to validate a policy set before activation and to fall back visibly to the last valid set, as the PSA asks.

    Evidence, impact and proposal
    • Type: gap (availability and lifecycle)
    • Trigger: SL-44 (PR #208). A stale policy declared the same Rego package as an existing rule.
    • Observation:
      • The PDP compiles the policy set as a whole. One bad module caused HTTP 500 on every request. The PDP returned no deny.
      • Upstream, a delete fails on a foreign key to the audit trail. The way out is to retire the policy: remove the bundle tag (seed-openftv-manager.py#L116-L122).
      • The same class of fault came back three times:
        • Non-Rego files silently dropped policies (PR #228).
        • A PDP looked healthy but got 403 on every bundle fetch (PR #337).
        • A module that does not compile is reported as loaded, while the old policy keeps deciding (demo issue #151).
      • As a local safety net, make policy-check runs before every PDP start (PR #372, Makefile#L92). Reconciliation with git is built. Validation and fallback at activation are not built.
    • Why it matters: the policy set is one availability object. One bad rule affects the whole authorization decision. PSA S06 sets the norm: tests before publication, a controlled fallback, and a rollback procedure. Source metadata already has this lifecycle (F-59).
    • Proposal: RL-04 (retire, reconcile) and RL-05 (validate before activation, visible fallback to the last valid set). The point was reported to the OpenFTV maintainers. Q-21 puts PAP and PDP at each source. Bundle scoping per policy owner therefore matters only where sources share a PDP.
    • Related: RL-04, RL-05, REQ-66, F-09, F-51, F-59, Q-21, ADR-0021.

    F-54: Log masking in the policy engine is inert, so the BSN-free decision log depended on input construction¶

    • Status: open

    The policy engine loads a masking rule for its decision log but never applies it, so BSN values reached the log in clear text. The demo then replaced the BSN with a pseudonym in the PDP input, until it removed that step on purpose. In demo v0.9.0, the EUDI and ASI routes put the plain BSN in the PDP input and in the logs. The proposal keeps identifiers out of the PDP input when the input is built. Masking stays allowed only as a second line, with a continuous test on real log output.

    Evidence, impact and proposal
    • Type: replaced assumption
    • Trigger: the harvest of SL-44. The masking rule from SL-29 did not exist any more.
    • Observation:
      • In the OpenFTV PDP, a system.log masking rule loads. The decision-log plugin never applies it (demo issue #149). Values reached Loki in clear text. Bundle distribution does not change this.
      • The PDP mapper then carried the guarantee: it turned the wallet BSN into a PI before evaluation.
      • PR #370 removed that step on purpose. In demo v0.9.0, the EUDI and ASI routes put the plain BSN in the PDP input, the ADL, and the console log (CHANGELOG.md#L598-L602). The DvTP path carries only a placeholder.
    • Why it matters: PSA P-12 and S09 ask for minimal logging. From the outside, a masking rule that does not work looks the same as one that works. The EUDI and ASI routes do not meet REQ-60.
    • Proposal: demo issue #368 decides between masking and transforming first. REQ-60 prefers minimization at input construction. Masking is allowed only as a second line, with a continuous test on real log output. ADR-0043 (proposed) records this choice.
    • Related: REQ-60, REQ-20, REQ-71, F-37, F-30, ADR-0043.

    F-57: The source owns its service description, and GBO validates and activates it¶

    • Status: open

    Each source publishes one service description that names its data service, query, parameters, mapping, attestation schema, and offer. GBO finds the source through its valid contracts, validates the description, and activates it in one step. The issuance configuration and the offers in the frontend come from this description, so a source can join without a change in GBO code. For the EUDI branch, this design gives the GO service catalogue a concrete form, which the bronhouder manages with a common profile. Three points stay open: a link to the DCAT-AP-NL description, central findability, and a conflict with the wish for no fixed DvTP queries.

    Evidence, impact and proposal
    • Type: directional design choice
    • Trigger: SL-46 (PR #219, PR #224). Before this demo increment, every added source, attestation type, or year needed a change in GBO code or in a GBO catalog file.
    • Observation:
      • Each source publishes one document at /.well-known/gbo (source_configuration.go#L15). The document holds the data service name, endpoint, query, parameters, mapping, attestation schema, and offer.
      • The reconciler finds sources through valid contracts. It validates schema, origin, version, validity, and contract binding. Then it activates the document atomically.
      • The issuance configuration and the frontend offers are generated from this document.
      • Two sources (BD, BRP) use the same path, without fallback.
    • Why it matters: the GO has two catalogs: a data catalog that follows DCAT-AP-NL, and a service catalog. The service catalog lists the allowed data requests. It is "managed federatively with a common profile and central findability", and the bronhouder manages it (GO §6). /.well-known/gbo is that service catalog, and the validation profile of the reconciler is the common profile. This design gives P-10 a concrete form. It answers Q-19 for the EUDI branch.
      1. The description does not refer to the DCAT-AP-NL description of the data set or data service. This point is an addition to REQ-64.
      2. Central findability is missing. It belongs with the catalog of the management organization.
      3. "Allowed data requests per application" conflicts with the wish for no fixed DvTP queries (Q-14).

      Proposal: keep REQ-64. Three points stay open:

      The demo proves the approach on the EUDI branch. F-74 covers DvTP. Semantics stay out of scope: a source-owned mapping is not a canonical model (Q-11). The form of the document is the input of the demo to the open PSA S10 decision.

    • Related: REQ-64, REQ-65, REQ-66, AV-07, F-21, F-23, F-71, F-74, Q-11, Q-14, Q-19, Q-38, ADR-0016.

    F-68: A chain read goes forward only, and in DvTP the chain starts at a party that LDV does not bind¶

    • Status: open

    A chain read in LDV starts at the party that started the processing and follows pointers forward to each next logbook. In DvTP, a private consumer starts the processing, and LDV does not bind that party, so nobody knows which sources one processing touched. The PDP at the source also writes only decision records, so no record at the source points to the consent logbook. The proposal uses the consent register as an index: every retrieval leads to a status lookup there, and the register logs each lookup. The step from the source to the register is open with Logius.

    Evidence, impact and proposal
    • Type: design gap
    • Trigger: demo issue #365. The chain view in the developer portal knew a chain only because all logbooks were in its configuration. No record in the DvTP flow carried dpl.read.nextLogbookId.
    • Observation:
      • The LDV read extension always starts at the application that started the processing. From there, it follows nextLogbookId. A caller of another party records the read API of that party. dpl.core.foreign_operation.processor names the caller, but it gives no route back.
      • Point 1 is the start of the chain. In DvTP, a private consumer starts the processing. LDV is meant for organizations with a public task. Without the records of the consumer, no one knows which sources one processing touched.
      • Point 2 is the step from the source to the register. The PDP at the source does the status lookup. A PDP writes ADL records only (F-63). The ADL has no equivalent of nextLogbookId, so no record at the source points to the consent logbook.
      • In demo v0.9.0 (PR #366), the consumer has its own logbook with nextLogbookId per source call. The chain view starts at the start logbooks and follows the pointers. It shows the consent logbook as "reached without pointer".
    • Why it matters: PSA S09, FR-20, and FR-31 ask for a chain audit and citizen access. If nobody can follow a chain, nobody can audit it.
    • Proposal:
      • Point 1 has an outcome without the private consumer. FR-31 v0.9 puts the chain audit at the facility. Every retrieval at any source leads to a status lookup at the register, with trace, consent_id, and consumer. The register logs each lookup as an LDV processing.
      • As long as the status is fetched per request, these records form a chain-wide index (REQ-75). Since demo issue #383, the caller of the lookup is verified over FSC (REQ-76).
      • From the consumer, the minimum is trace context (AV-01). LDV for the consumer is a candidate (AV-02). Q-24 is closed.
      • Point 2 is open with Logius: Q-25.
      • REQ-64: if the source does not publish its read API, a caller cannot fill nextLogbookId.
    • Related: F-65, F-76, REQ-72, REQ-75, REQ-76, AV-01, AV-02, Q-24, Q-25, ADR-0026, ADR-0036.

    F-70: The regime follows from the proof, but only consent is positive proof: the PID regime comes from not sending something¶

    • Status: open

    The PDP picks the rules from the evidence in a request. A consent proof is positive evidence: it is signed, and the PDP checks it. The PID regime has no evidence of its own. A request ends up there because it carries no consent proof. So the only guard on that regime is a short list of callers, and the adapter does not check who calls it. The design needs a verified PID as positive evidence for this regime.

    Evidence, impact and proposal
    • Type: exposed design choice and design gap
    • Trigger: PR #363 (closes demo issue #334) removed flow as a grant property and as authorization input. PR #370 and PR #371 completed the change: the policy reads the proof itself.
    • Observation:
      • One signal sets the regime: a consent token in the request, verified or not. With a token, the consent regime applies. Without one, the PID regime applies (engine.rego#L168-L175). The policy ignores a pip.consent that comes from outside.
      • A request cannot carry evidence for both regimes. For this reason, the earlier AMBIGUOUS_EVIDENCE rule is gone. The deny reason comes from the rule that got furthest through its own steps.
      • The only control on the PID regime is the list of designated issuers per EUDI rule (eudi_helper.rego#L13-L24). If a consumer OIN is on that list, that consumer can skip consent by leaving out the token.
      • A test requires the issuer list to be disjoint from the DvTP consumers (engine_test.rego#L371). The test knows only the local consumers. The simulation consumer (0000009950HYPBV00000) is not in the test. For that consumer, disjointness rests on inspection, and no test covers it.
      • The PID step checks only that there is no consent token and that the query names a citizen. A comment in the code says so: "The PID itself is not verified here; the adapter trusts the disclosure." The adapter does not authenticate the callback from the issuance server (demo issue #133, open).
    • Why it matters: the DvTP regime rests on a signed artifact that the PDP verifies (REQ-68). The EUDI regime rests on an identifier whose origin no one in the chain checks. The design must say what the evidence for the EUDI legal basis is. If it does not, the regime stays defined as "no consent proof". Since PR #363, a place on the PID actor list is an admission decision with authorization weight (Q-22).
    • Proposal: REQ-74 and ADR-0031 record the choice. The structural fix is positive PID evidence. That evidence is made where the wallet presentation is verified, and it is bound to the subject of the source query (REQ-45, demo issues #133 and #381). Q-18 covers the legal status of the BSN from the PID. Until then, test the disjointness in each environment as well as locally.
    • Related: F-62, F-90, REQ-45, REQ-48, REQ-68, REQ-74, Q-15, Q-18, Q-22, ADR-0031.

    F-71: A source without its own metadata endpoint: the operator takes over its service description¶

    • Status: open

    Some sources publish only their data service and no service description of their own. For such a source, the demo lets the operator keep the description in a directory. The operator then decides the query, the mapping, and the wallet claims for the product of another party. Four bounds stay in place, among them the pinned contract and the check of the OIN against the certificate. The design allows this only as an exception, with the recorded agreement of the bronhouder as first condition, and that condition is not built.

    Evidence, impact and proposal
    • Type: exposed design choice (exception to F-57)
    • Trigger: PR #318. An external source in the simulation published only its GraphQL service on FSC. It did not publish /.well-known/gbo. With one transport for both legs, the demo had no way to connect such a source.
    • Observation:
      • In demo v0.9.0, metadata and data each have their own transport. A file transport reads the description from a directory that the operator keeps (reconcile_command.go#L192, --metadata-dir). The registry reports per leg whether its transport is authenticated.
      • The demo README states this openly. The operator who keeps the file takes over the description of the product of another party. That operator must record who agreed to it (source-metadata/README.md#L17-L22).
      • Four bounds stay in place:
        • the peer ID from the own configuration
        • the grant hash that pins the contract
        • the OIN check against the certificate
        • promotion as a separate step
    • Why it matters: F-57 and REQ-64 put the description at the source (PSA P-10). Whoever controls the document decides the query, the mapping, and the wallet claims. With file transport, the operator controls the document. The origin check then falls back to the OIN in the certificate.
    • Proposal: the design is in REQ-64, as an exception clause, and in AV-08. Its first condition is the recorded agreement of the bronhouder. That condition is not built. Demo issue #221 (open) covers the trust model per transport, including an unsecured variant. According to the Q-06 review, this is no governance question.
    • Related: F-57, F-58, REQ-64, REQ-65, AV-08, Q-06, ADR-0016.

    F-72: The decision record can carry only what the consumer also gets to see¶

    • Status: open

    The ADL is the audit record of every decision. It stores the answer that the PDP gives to the consumer, and nothing else. That answer is deliberately coarse, so the ADL is coarse too: it holds no reason per field and no consent status. A supervisor can therefore reconstruct only what the consumer was allowed to know, and nobody can replay a decision from the record.

    Evidence, impact and proposal
    • Type: exposed design choice
    • Trigger: PR #373 (closes demo issue #332) made the ADL the audit source. The change had to choose how decision detail gets into the ADL.
    • Observation:
      • OpenFTV reads only allow and reason from the policy. reason becomes reason_user.en in the AuthZEN answer. The ADL stores that answer unchanged (policies/authz.rego#L42-L48). Per-field detail and the step trace are only in the console log of the engine, and that log is observability.

What a supervisor must reconstruct is then limited to what the consumer is allowed to know (PSA P-12, S09, FR-31). Since PR #371, the policy fetches the consent status during evaluation. If the record does not hold that answer, nobody can replay the decision (F-61).

    • Type: exposed design choice

    F-75: The BSNk design fits the specification, but the consent facility can fetch the values only as a recognized party¶

    • Status: open

    BSNk polymorphic pseudonymization is mandatory for DvTP. A check against the BSNk specifications showed that the design fits on five points and keeps BSNk out of the request path. One point does not fit: only a recognized BSNk party can activate and transform values. The citizen logs in at MijnOverheid before the sources are known, so the login cannot deliver the values. The remaining work is administrative and legal: the consent facility (toestemmingsvoorziening) must become a recognized party, and someone must decide who the BSNk participant is.

    Evidence, impact and proposal
    • Type: assumption (checked), with one gap
    • Trigger: the BSNk technical specifications v11.2 (December 2025) and the decryption component documentation, read next to the design in demo issue #364 (September 30, 2026).
    • Observation:
      • Five parts of the design fit the specification:
        • An encrypted value per party in the proof is the recipient-specific BSNk form, per OIN (BSNK-07).
        • The choice between identity and pseudonym by authorization is a lookup in the BSN authorization list (Autorisatielijst BSN) (BSNK-06).
        • Transformation happens when the citizen gives consent, the only time when BSNk allows it (BSNK-04).
        • Local decryption at the source, after the PDP, is what every BSNk recipient does (BSNK-09).
        • The design makes no BSNk call at query time, so BSNk stays out of the query path.
      • One part does not fit: only a recognized party is allowed to activate and transform (BSNK-01). The login cannot deliver the values, because the citizen logs in at MijnOverheid before the sources are known.
      • Three earlier assumptions were wrong:
        • A polymorphic identity is specific to the requester, not to the recipient (BSNK-03).
        • The closing key comes from BSNk, not from the recipient.
        • PI and PP do not have the same starting point.
      • The demo built three parts on the BSNk mock (SL-51, PR #469, PR #477):
        • an encrypted pseudonym for every source
        • an encrypted identity only for a source on the BSN authorization list
        • requests of at most four parties (BSNK-05)
      • The spike on the real BSNk PP (demo issue #377) closed on October 21, 2026. Its result: the mock follows the BSNk interface and rules. Real activation, transport, key handling, and the agreement for private recipients wait for pre-production access.
    • Why it matters: PSA P-04, S01, B-07, and GO §5 make BSNk PP mandatory for DvTP. Nothing technical blocks the design. The remaining work is administrative and legal. The consent facility must become a recognized BSNk party, in a role that BSNk was not written for.
    • Proposal: use BSNK-01 to BSNK-11 as the check for each build step and for the trial with the real BSNk PP. Submit the recognition question (Q-26). Decide who the BSNk participant is (Q-49, ADR-0040, proposed). Two points stay open in the demo: a pseudonym for the consumer (demo issue #478), and how the BSN authorization list is delivered in practice (Q-26).

    F-76: The consumer has no citizen identifier, so what it records about "this person" depends on the consent¶

    • Status: open

    The service provider never learns who the citizen is. It holds a consent proof and names the citizen with a placeholder. This goes further than "no BSN for private parties", and it has side effects. The service provider logs under the consent ID, so one citizen with two consents is two people in its logbook. It cannot tell that two consents come from the same person, and it can answer a request for access only through the consents.

    Evidence, impact and proposal
    • Type: exposed design choice
    • Trigger: SL-51 (PR #469). The proof carries an encrypted identity per source instead of a PI. The consumer cannot read it. A pseudonym of its own (VP) for the consumer is optional in the design. It is not built (demo issue #478).
    • Observation:
      • The consumer reads only consent_id and scopes from the proof. It names the subject with a placeholder.
      • Its logbook names the data subject by the consent ID, under the subject type consent-id (logbook.go#L22).
      • In one query chain, the same citizen is c-… at the consumer, LP-… at the source, and EP-… at the consent register (seen in the running demo, September 30, 2026).
    • Why it matters: PSA P-04 says "BSN-free for private consumers". This design goes further: the consumer is identifier-free. The design does not name three effects of this choice:
      1. Logging: REQ-72 asks for a pseudonym from the own space of the controller. The consumer has none. A consent ID is no pseudonym. One citizen with two consents is two data subjects in the consumer logbook.
      2. Access: if a citizen asks the consumer what it processed about them, the consumer can find that citizen only through their consents. The consent register translates the citizen to consents (FR-31, F-68).
      3. Recognition: through GBO, the consumer cannot tell that two consents are from the same person. That is data minimization (PSA P-02), but it is a design choice. With a VP, the consumer can tell, and it then has a pseudonym to log under.
    • Proposal: add a rule to REQ-72 for a party without an identifier of the data subject. That party logs under the reference to the legal basis, with its own data_subject_id_type. Submit the question whether that rule fits LDV (Q-27). Make "VP for the consumer or not" an explicit design choice, with these three effects (Q-01).
    • Related: F-69, F-75, F-77, REQ-20, REQ-72, AV-02, Q-01, Q-27.

    F-77: The PDP checks that the subject comes from the proof, and no longer who the subject is¶

    • Status: open

    The PDP used to compare the citizen in the query with the citizen in the consent. Now the query carries a placeholder, and the proof carries a value that the PDP cannot read. The PDP checks only that the citizen comes from the proof. A rule can therefore never depend on a property of the citizen, and one request can be about one citizen only.

    Evidence, impact and proposal
    • Type: exposed design choice (replaces the subject binding of the removed REQ-27)
    • Trigger: SL-51 (PR #469). Before this demo increment, the policy compared the PI in the query with the PI in the proof. After it, the query has a placeholder, and the proof has a value that the PDP cannot read.
    • Observation:
      • Since PR #476, two placeholders exist, one for each form that an API takes: consent:identity (the BSN) and consent:pseudonym (the source's own pseudonym) (lib.rego#L49-L53). Each rule names the placeholders that it accepts. The PDP denies any other value with CONSTRAINT_MISMATCH, before the request reaches the source.
      • Without a verified proof, a placeholder stands for nobody. A value from the request itself does not change that.
      • The PDP denies a literal BSN with CONSTRAINT_MISMATCH, also when the proof is valid.
      • The PDP sees only the placeholder in the query.
    • Why it matters: the binding of the query to the legal basis (PSA S05) changed from a comparison into a construction. The only subject that the source can fill in is the subject from the proof. This change has four effects:
      1. The PDP has no subject-dependent policy, because it does not know who the person is. If a policy depends on a property of the citizen, it must get that property from the proof. Otherwise it must run at the source after the substitution.
      2. A request has one subject: one placeholder per form and one citizen per consent. A question about two persons (partner, child) does not fit. This point stays open. Demo issue #473 closed without a solution for it.
      3. Substitution works only on variables. If the query text holds a literal placeholder, that placeholder passes the PDP, and nothing replaces it. This fails closed: the source finds no one.
      4. The decision log has no identifier, but it holds the proof with the encrypted value. In the demo, that value is readable because the mock does not encrypt (demo issue #368).
    • Proposal: REQ-79 holds the requirement. Record in ADR-0033 that, under a consent, the binding is the rule "the subject comes from the proof". The binding is no comparison of identifiers. Point 2 needs a design decision.
    • Related: F-44, F-76, F-79, REQ-21, REQ-79, REQ-80, ADR-0033.

    F-78: The proof freezes at consent: parties, key versions, and proof form are fixed for the whole validity period¶

    • Status: open

    At consent, the portal makes an encrypted value for each source, with the OIN and the key version of that source. The consent proof therefore fixes the parties, the key versions, the BSN authorization, and the proof form for its whole validity period. An added party needs another consent from the citizen. A changed OIN or proof form can break outstanding proofs, and a consent can be valid for months. The proposal adds the OIN, the key version, and the BSN authorization per party to the service catalogue, and gives the proof a version.

    Evidence, impact and proposal
    • Type: design gap
    • Trigger: SL-51 (PR #469). To make the values, the portal must know an OIN and a key version per source. No part of the chain holds these values, so they became configuration.
    • Observation:
      • The portal gets the sources as <OIN>@<key set version> from an environment variable. Without at least one source, the portal gives no consent (consent-portal-backend/main.go#L154-L173).
      • The source keeps its keys as files at the source gateway. In the demo, the keys are in the image.
      • Since PR #477, the portal reads the BSN authorization from the BSN authorization list of BSNk instead of from configuration (portal.go#L38). The authorization is therefore a snapshot too. If a source joins the list after the consent, that consent holds only a pseudonym for the source.
      • A proof in an older form is denied with CONSENT_CONTEXT_INVALID. The proof form changed twice (PR #469, PR #477). Each time, older proofs stopped working.
    • Why it matters: BSNK-04 (transformation only while the citizen is present) makes the proof a snapshot. After each later change, either the old proof keeps working, or the citizen gives a new consent. Each kind of change has its own effect:
      1. A new party needs a new consent (demo issue #382).
      2. A new certificate at a source gives a new key version. While proofs for the old keys are valid, the source keeps those keys (BSNK-08, AV-09). The register must know the new version before the next consent (demo issue #467).
      3. If a source gets a different OIN, the proof stops working for that source.
      4. If the PDP and the source do not keep the old form, a different proof form makes all outstanding proofs lapse. FR-11 allows months of validity, and the proof has no version. - The proof keys values on the OIN, while FSC knows parties by the Peer ID (F-60, Q-68). For each party of a service, the register must know the OIN, the key version, and the BSN authorization (BSNK-06). The service catalogue (FR-32, F-74) does not carry these values.
    • Proposal: REQ-82 holds the requirement. Extend the service catalogue with the OIN, the key version, and the BSN authorization per party. Give the proof a version. Record how long an old version stays valid. Q-77 covers what counts as a change of a consent.
    • Related: F-60, F-74, F-75, REQ-82, AV-09, Q-68, Q-76, Q-77, ADR-0032.

    F-79: "After the decision" holds only if nothing but the Inway reaches the source gateway¶

    • Status: open (the demo fixes it for Docker Compose. The roll-out outside Compose and the nonce are open.)

    The source gateway decrypts the citizen from the consent proof and does not check the proof again. That is safe only when every request at the gateway has passed the PDP first. In an early demo version a consumer was able to reach the gateway directly and skip the PDP. The demo now isolates the gateway on its own networks. Every production deployment needs the same isolation, and a nonce can bind a value to one consent as a second guard.

    Evidence, impact and proposal
    • Type: design gap (security)
    • Trigger: SL-51 (PR #469). The substitution runs in the source gateway behind the Inway. A plugin in the Inway after the authorization needs a custom Inway build (demo issue #364). A review of PR #469 showed that consumers were able to reach the source gateway without the Inway.
    • Observation:
      • The source gateway reads the proof without a signature check. It relies on the PDP that checked the same request (substitution.go#L105-L107). The source gateway does check two things: the proof has a value for its own OIN, and the BSNk-signed value names that OIN as recipient.
      • Before the fix, consumers shared networks with the source gateways, and the source gateways published a port. A consumer with a real proof was able to send any query to the source gateway directly. The source gateway decrypted the identity and passed the query on. The checks on scope, fields, actor, and revocation did not run.
      • The fix: the source gateways publish no port. Each source gateway is only on a network with the Inways of the source and on a network with the service behind the source (docker-compose.yml#L829-L836). A check on October 5, 2026 showed that consumer networks and the host cannot reach the source gateways. The Inways can reach them.
    • Why it matters:
      • The rule "only after a positive decision" (REQ-21) depends on the deployment. The source gateway alone cannot make it true.
      • A signature check in the source gateway does not help. A consumer holds real proofs, so a direct call with a real proof passes the check and still skips the PDP.
      • Only two measures help: isolation, or a proof of the decision that is bound to the request. The Inway does not pass such a proof on.
      • A BSNk value is bound to a recipient. It is not bound to a consent, and it has no end date. A nonce can bind it to one consent as a second line of defense (PSA S05).
    • Proposal: REQ-81 says that only the component that enforces the decision reaches the source gateway. Outside Compose, this needs a NetworkPolicy or an equivalent measure. Add the nonce as a second line of defense (BSNk preconditions). The nonce is not built. Both measures are open design points from demo issue #473.
    • Related: F-37, F-77, REQ-21, REQ-68, REQ-81, Q-14, ADR-0033.

    F-80: Since 2026 the ASI has a statutory interface and a seal obligation, but the authorization part stays a free choice¶

    • Status: open

    Since August 2026, EU law requires an authentic source interface (ASI) for the verification of attributes. From January 1, 2027, the interface must follow ETSI TS 119 478, and the source or a designated intermediary must seal every result. The ETSI part on authorization is not designated, so the member state chooses how the citizen is identified and how the request is authorized. The GO and the PSA do not say that verification is a legal obligation or that every result needs a seal. The demo seals results with a development certificate, and who seals, in which format, and who is the intermediary stay open.

    Evidence, impact and proposal
    • Type: external requirement (regulation)
    • Trigger: a reading of the 34 EUDI implementing acts (October 5 to 6, 2026), and then of the ARF v3.0.0 and its technical specifications.
    • Observation:
      • CIR (EU) 2025/1569, art. 9, applies from August 19, 2026. It requires an access point, attributes plus identification data in the request, and a result that says whether the attribute is verified.
      • CIR (EU) 2026/1735 adds art. 9(2a): the mechanism follows Annex IV. Annex IV designates ETSI TS 119 478 V1.1.1 clause 6.1.1 (verify over HTTP), clause 6.2.3 (verify over ISO 15000), or both.
      • The same act replaces art. 9(4): the result "shall be signed or sealed" by the public sector body or the designated intermediary. The minimum is an advanced signature or seal on a qualified certificate. On Match or MatchWithVariation, the result can contain the value.
      • Art. 9(2a) and 9(4) apply from January 1, 2027.
      • Annex IV does not designate clause 6.1.3 (the OAuth authorization server, with the identification data in a JWT token) or clause 5 (discover). CIR 2026/1735 also declares ETSI TS 119 471 clause 4.2.2.2 not applicable. As a result, QTSPs verify only through art. 9.
      • The ARF (QTSPAS_02) asks the source or intermediary to establish on each request that the requester is a QTSP that issues QEAAs. The ARF gives an example: "for example by means of a lookup in the QTSP Trusted List". The verification point is registered in the catalogue of attributes, as a DataService per source.
      • Demo state (SL-28): the ASIP seals every successful result with a detached JWS over the body, with the chain in x5c (REQ-83, PR #499, seal.go). The certificate is a development certificate. It is not qualified. The authorization follows clause 6.1.3 (REQ-86 to REQ-89).
    • Why it matters: the GO and PSA S11 (B-14) name an ASI provider per ETSI TS 119 478. They do not say that the verify part becomes a legal obligation, or that every result must be sealed. The member state chooses how the citizen is identified and how the request is authorized.
    • Proposal:
      • Add three points to PSA S11 and B-14. First, the verify interface follows ETSI clause 6.1.1, clause 6.2.3, or both. Second, every result is sealed with a qualified seal certificate. Third, the authorization is a free choice.
      • The demo choice for the authorization is ADR-0037 (clause 6.1 only, REQ-112, Q-64).
      • The seal is open: who seals (Q-17) and in which format (Q-32, ADR-0041, proposed).
      • Three more points are open: who is designated as intermediary (Q-28), whether the value is returned (Q-29), and who registers the verification points (Q-31).

    F-82: An issuer must track changes in the source, but GBO only supports queries¶

    • Status: open

    The ARF requires that a provider revokes an attestation when an attribute value in the source changes. The rule applies to revocable attestations that are valid for 24 hours or more. GBO supports only queries, and in the demo the source sends no change notifications. A provider of long-lived attestations therefore needs a notification stream from the source. The proposal is a choice per attestation type: short-lived attestations that the provider issues again often, or long-lived attestations with change notifications from the source.

    Evidence, impact and proposal
    • Type: external requirement (ARF)
    • Trigger: a reading of the ARF v3.0.0 (October 6, 2026).
    • Observation: ARF VCR_09 says that a provider "SHALL revoke that PID or attestation if the value of one or more of the attributes … was changed". This rule applies to a revocable attestation that is valid for 24 hours or more. An attestation that is valid for less than 24 hours does not have to be revocable. If an authentic source sets the value, "the Provider is responsible for ensuring that this third party notifies them about such changes".
    • Why it matters: GBO is designed for queries. In the demo, the adapter fetches the data from the source at issuance. The source sends no change notifications. A provider of long-lived PuB-EAAs needs such a notification stream. The alternative is short-lived attestations (less than 24 hours) that the provider issues again often.
    • Proposal: add a choice per attestation type to PSA S11 and B-13. The two options are short-lived attestations that the provider issues again often, or long-lived attestations with a change notification stream from the source. The ARF is not binding, but the requirement follows from the duty to revoke on changed data. REQ-77, AV-12, and AV-13 cover the validity and revocation policy per bronhouder.
    • Related: F-81, REQ-77, AV-12, AV-13, ADR-0018.

    F-83: Approval in the wallet is not a legal basis, so the EUDI route and DvTP consent cannot lean on each other¶

    • Status: open

    The ARF states that approval in the wallet is never a lawful ground for processing by the relying party. In DvTP, the consent of the citizen is the legal basis for delivery to a private party. In the EUDI route, the citizen chooses only what to present. A consumer that uses both routes therefore needs its own legal basis for the wallet route. The proposal separates the terms in the GO and the PSA: consent is a registered, revocable legal basis, and approval is a choice per presentation.

    Evidence, impact and proposal
    • Type: exposed assumption
    • Trigger: a reading of the ARF v3.0.0 (October 6, 2026).
    • Observation:
      • ARF §6.6.3.5.1 is explicit about "User approval to present data from their Wallet Unit". Under no circumstances is this approval "lawful grounds for the processing of personal data by the Relying Party or any other entity".
      • A relying party must have its own ground under GDPR art. 6.
      • eIDAS art. 5b(9) and ARF §2.5.1 add a second rule. If the law does not require identification, a relying party cannot refuse a pseudonym.
    • Why it matters: in DvTP, the consent of the citizen is the legal basis for delivery to a private party (PSA P-01, S01). In the EUDI route, the citizen chooses in the wallet what to present. The ARF explicitly says that this choice is something else. A consumer that can get data through DvTP and through the wallet needs its own legal basis for the wallet route. No single "consent" covers both routes. The same holds for the ASI: the citizen approves by logging in per request, and that approval is no stored, revocable consent (REQ-90).
    • Proposal: separate the two terms in the GO and the PSA. Consent in DvTP is the legal basis, registered and revocable. Approval in the wallet is the choice of the citizen per presentation, and it is no legal basis.
    • Related: F-70, REQ-68, REQ-90, ADR-0032.

    F-84: ETSI leaves the security profile of the ASI authorization server open, and conflicts on two points with the norms it refers to¶

    • Status: open

    ETSI leaves the security profile of the ASI authorization server largely open. It gives a choice for client authentication and for token binding, and it only asks to take the FAPI 2.0 profile into account. On two points, ETSI conflicts with the norms that it refers to: pushed authorization requests and the RS256 algorithm. A QTSP that verifies at several ASI providers must therefore implement a different profile for each authorization server. The proposal is one fixed profile for Dutch ASI providers in the PSA, and a report of the two conflicts to ETSI.

    Evidence, impact and proposal
    • Type: open design choice
    • Trigger: SL-28, the authorization server of the ASIP mock (demo issue #503).
    • Observation:
      • REQ-AZSP-6.1.3.1-05 and -06 give a choice per mechanism. Client authentication uses mTLS or private_key_jwt. Token binding uses mTLS or DPoP. The demo chose private_key_jwt and DPoP.
      • REQ-AZSP-6.1.3.4-01 only asks to take the FAPI 2.0 Security Profile "into account". NOTE 3 at the PKCE requirement refers to FAPI clause 5.3.2.2, which requires pushed authorization requests (PAR, RFC 9126). ETSI does not require PAR. The demo does not offer PAR, because a server that requires PAR refuses QTSPs that implement only ETSI.
      • REQ-AZSP-6.1.3.1-08 makes RFC 9068 §2.1 mandatory. RFC 9068 §2.1 requires resource servers to support RS256. FAPI 2.0 allows only PS256, ES256, and EdDSA. The authorization server signs with ES256. The ASIP also accepts RS256.
      • PR #574 weighed RFC 9700 and FAPI 2.0 item by item (conformance/security-considerations.md). The absence of PAR and the support for RS256 are deliberate demo choices. Two points stay open: iss in the authorization response (F-96) and tokens after a right is revoked (Q-41).
    • Why it matters: a QTSP that verifies at ASIPs in several member states, or at several Dutch ASIPs, must implement a different profile per authorization server. The GO and the PSA do not name the authorization. F-80 found that the member state is free to choose it.
    • Proposal: fix one profile for Dutch ASIPs in the PSA (S11, B-14). The profile names the client authentication, the token binding, and whether PAR is required (Q-66). Send the two conflicts to ETSI (E8).
    • Related: F-80, F-96, REQ-87, Q-41, Q-66, E8, E10, ADR-0037.

    • Status: open

No text says which identifier links the QTSP certificate to its register entry, and ETSI does not say what happens when the register is unreachable. The demo uses a stub register and test certificates, and it keeps a client active when the register is unreachable.     • Type: external dependency
    • Trigger: SL-28, dynamic client registration (PR #522).
    • Observation:

    F-86: ETSI requires the identification data in the token, and without encryption the QTSP reads the BSN¶

    • Status: open

    ETSI requires that the access token of the ASI carries the identification data of the citizen, and it rejects token introspection. The QTSP holds that token. In the demo, the token carries the BSN in readable form, together with the name, birth date, and nationality. Without encryption, the QTSP therefore processes the BSN, while the DvTP route never sends a readable BSN as a field. The proposal is that only the ASI provider can read the identification data, for example through a claim that is encrypted for the provider.

    Evidence, impact and proposal
    • Type: conflict with a principle
    • Trigger: SL-28, the access token of the authorization server (demo issue #503).
    • Observation:
      • REQ-AZSP-6.1.3.1-08 requires JWT tokens "which shall contain the required identification data of the user". NOTE 6 rejects token introspection, because it does not meet art. 9(3) of CIR 2025/1569.
      • RFC 9068 §6 names encryption of the token or of sensitive claims as a privacy measure.
      • The demo carries the BSN readable in the claim https://gbo.example/claims/bsn (tokens.go#L29). Since demo issue #523, the token also carries the mandatory PID attributes that the identification means supplies (REQ-86). Demo issue #595 covers the protection and is open.
    • Why it matters: in the DvTP route, no readable BSN travels as a field (REQ-20, REQ-21). In the ASI route, the QTSP holds the token. Without encryption, the QTSP processes the BSN (PSA P-04). It also processes the name, birth date, and nationality from the identification. The QTSP often has those data from the citizen already, but the proposal covers the whole claim set. The same question applies to the encrypted identity that a consumer sends on (Q-01).
    • Proposal: record in the PSA (S11) that only the ASIP can read the identification data in the token. One way is a claim that is encrypted for the ASIP. Send the gap to ETSI (E7).
    • Related: F-88, F-93, REQ-20, REQ-86, Q-01, Q-36, E7.

    F-87: In the ASI route the QTSP is not an FSC participant, and the generic chain starts behind the ASIP¶

    • Status: open

    The earlier design assumed that a QTSP verifies through FSC as an OIN holder. Under the designated ETSI route, the QTSP talks to the ASI provider over HTTP as an OAuth client, and FSC has no part in admission. The generic GBO chain starts behind the ASI provider, which is the actor towards the source. The source sees neither the QTSP nor the citizen, and the citizen cannot see QTSP verifications in any logbook. Open points are which data about the QTSP the provider passes into the chain, and how the source gets proof of the citizen.

    Evidence, impact and proposal
    • Type: replaces an assumption
    • Trigger: SL-28. The earlier design assumed a QTSP that verifies through an FSC Outway as an OIN holder.
    • Observation:
      • ETSI TS 119 478 clause 6.1 is the designated route since CIR 2026/1735. In this route, the QTSP talks to the ASIP over HTTP as an OAuth client. It uses a token from the authorization server of the ASIP (clause 6.1.3). Admission runs through the register of relying parties and the QTSP certificate (F-85). FSC has no part in admission. The demo builds it that way (REQ-86 to REQ-88).
      • The chain behind the ASIP exists (demo issue #525). The ASIP is an FSC peer of its own (…1200) with a contract for the BRP service. The ASIP is the actor in the chain: rule ASI0001 gives the ASIP peer the fields behind its attributes, on brp only (asi0001.rego).
      • The ASIP passes nothing about the QTSP into the chain. The source sees the ASIP. It does not see the QTSP or the citizen, and it relies on the ASIP for the check of the token.
      • The ASIP writes no LDV records, although it is a GBO-delivered component under REQ-72.
      • The PDP input and the decision log hold the BSN on ASI requests too (REQ-60, demo issue #368).
    • Why it matters: the ASI is a front door with its own trust model (PSA P-14). The generic chain stays reusable, with the ASIP as its actor instead of the QTSP. The citizen cannot see QTSP verifications in any logbook.
    • Proposal: ADR-0037 records the front door. Describe it the same way in the GO and the PSA (S11, B-14). Decide which data about the QTSP the ASIP passes into the chain, for policy and for processing logging. Proof of the citizen at the source (the encrypted BSN of part 9 of the demo epic) is open. ADR-0043 (proposed) keeps the BSN out of the PDP input.
    • Related: F-85, F-90, REQ-60, REQ-72, REQ-88, REQ-92, REQ-97, ADR-0029 (superseded), ADR-0037, ADR-0043.

    F-88: An identification means must deliver the mandatory identification data, or the data must come from elsewhere¶

    • Status: open

    ETSI requires the mandatory identification data of the citizen in the ASI token. The wallet PID delivers these data except the place of birth, but DigiD delivers only a BSN. If the Netherlands accepts such a means, the authorization server fetches the other data from the BRP, or the token holds only the BSN. The first option makes the server a BRP consumer, and the second needs a reading of "required identification data". The proposal is to record in the PSA which identification means the server accepts and which data the token carries for each means.

    Evidence, impact and proposal
    • Type: open design choice
    • Trigger: SL-28, request page and PID claims (demo issue #523). DigiD came up as a second login route next to the wallet.
    • Observation:
      • REQ-AZSP-6.1.3.1-08 requires "the required identification data of the user" in the token. REQ-AZSP-6.1.3.1-03 allows the EUDI wallet over OpenID4VP "or an alternative electronic identification procedure according to ETSI TS 119 461".
      • The wallet PID has the mandatory attributes, except place of birth (F-94). The demo copies them one to one (REQ-86, REQ-102).
      • DigiD supplies only a BSN. In demo v0.9.0, a DigiD mock is available next to the wallet, with only the BSN in the token (demo issue #544).
      • The other variant is not built: an authorization server that fetches the other data from the BRP. ETSI TS 119 461 allows this variant. With an eID means, a reliable register such as a population register can be supplementary evidence (USE-9.2.4-04 and its note).
    • Why it matters: if the Netherlands accepts a means that supplies only a BSN, two options remain. The authorization server must fetch the other data itself, or the token carries only the BSN. The first option makes the authorization server a BRP consumer. The second option needs a reading of "required identification data" (PSA P-04).
    • Proposal: record in the PSA (S11) which identification means the authorization server of a Dutch ASI accepts. Also record which identification data the token carries for each means (Q-36).

    F-89: ETSI lets a response name one authentic source, so an ASIP for several sources offers one data service per source¶

    • Status: open

    In ETSI, a response of the ASI can name only one authentic source. GBO builds one ASI provider for several sources, so one verify request with attributes from two sources does not fit one response. The demo therefore offers one data service per source, each with its own contract, catalogue entry, and token audience. A token is valid for one data service, so attributes from two sources need two authorizations and two citizen interactions. The proposal records this layout in the PSA and reports the gap to ETSI.

    Evidence, impact and proposal
    • Type: open design choice
    • Trigger: SL-28, part 4 of the demo epic (demo issue #525). GBO builds one ASIP for several authentic sources.
    • Observation:
      • REQ-ASIP-6.1.1.2-08 and REQ-ASIP-6.1.2.2-04 apply "if and only if the ASIP is different from the authentic source". In that case, the response has one authenticSource. The schema makes it a single object. CIR 2025/1569 art. 9(4) requires the result to name the responsible source, or the body that acts for it.
      • The Data Service Directory describes a dataService per attribute, with endpointURI and provider (REQ-DIP-5.3.3-03). One verify request with attributes of two sources does not fit one response.
      • The demo offers one data service per source under /<dataservice>/authsrc-api. Each data service has its own contract, catalogue entry, and token audience (REQ-94, REQ-95, PR #529, config/dataservices.json).
      • The reference QTSP follows this layout. It picks the data service that delivers all requested attributes. If no data service delivers all of them, the QTSP refuses the request before the authorization server and advises to split it (REQ-99, PR #535).
    • Why it matters: a token is valid for one data service. Attributes from two sources need two authorizations, with two citizen interactions. Q-37 asks whether one ASIP for several sources makes GBO an intermediary, or whether each bronhouder stays ASIP. The data-service layout fits both answers.
    • Proposal: the demo choice is ADR-0038 (accepted). Record three points in the PSA (S11, B-14): one data service per authentic source, the source in each response, and one token per data service. A possible improvement is several sources in one authorization (RFC 8707 §2.2, one token per resource). Send the gap to ETSI (E3).
    • Related: REQ-91, REQ-94, REQ-95, REQ-99, Q-37, E3, ADR-0038.

    F-91: ETSI requires the authorization-server metadata in the contract, but gives no field for it¶

    • Status: open

    ETSI requires that the contract of an ASI data service names the metadata of its OAuth authorization server. The OpenAPI format has no field for that metadata: its OAuth fields hold single URLs, and its OpenID field points to another kind of server. The demo uses its own extension field with the standard metadata URL. Without a fixed field, each ASI provider picks its own way, and a QTSP in several member states must learn each way. The proposal is to ask ETSI and, until ETSI answers, to describe the demo extension in the PSA as the Dutch choice.

    Evidence, impact and proposal
    • Type: open point in the specification
    • Trigger: SL-28, part 6 of the demo epic (demo issue #533, PR #534). The reference QTSP must find the authorization server from the Discover Interface record.
    • Observation:
      • REQ-DIP-5.3.3-03-02-01 requires that the contract that endpointDescription points to specifies the OAuth authorization-server metadata.
      • The OpenAPI 3.0 oauth2 security scheme has only authorizationUrl, tokenUrl, and refreshUrl. The openIdConnect type has openIdConnectUrl, but that URL points to OpenID Connect Discovery. The clause 6.1.3 server is an OAuth server with RFC 8414 metadata. It is no OpenID provider. As a result, no field exists for the metadata, which includes registration_endpoint for RFC 7591 and jwks_uri.
      • The demo uses the extension x-oauth-authorization-server, with the URL at the RFC 8414 §3.1 location (REQ-100, contract.go#L109).
    • Why it matters: without a fixed field, each ASIP chooses its own way. A QTSP that verifies in several member states must find out the way of each ASIP. The seal format has the same pattern (Q-32).
    • Proposal: submit the question to ETSI ESI (Q-39, E1). Until ETSI answers, describe the demo extension in the PSA (S11) as the Dutch choice.

    F-92: Annex IV of CIR 2026/1735 makes the value optional, but keeps the requirement for fuzzy matching¶

    • Status: open

    An EU implementing act makes the attribute value optional in a positive ASI result. It keeps an ETSI rule that an ASI provider with fuzzy matching must return the stored value. The demo does fuzzy matching and returns no value, so a literal reading says that the demo does not meet that rule. If the rule stays, a Dutch ASI provider must choose: return the stored value on a fuzzy match, or do no fuzzy matching. The proposal adds this choice to the open question whether the ASI returns the value, and reports the point to ETSI.

    Evidence, impact and proposal
    • Type: contradiction in the source text
    • Trigger: SL-28, part 6c of the demo epic (PR #540). The conformance matrix reads ETSI TS 119 478 as amended by Annex IV of CIR (EU) 2026/1735.
    • Observation:
      • Annex IV makes attributeValue optional on Match and MatchWithVariation (REQ-ASIP-6.1.1.2-04-01 to -03, and -06-04-01 to -03 for fragments).
      • Annex IV does not amend REQ-ASIP-6.1.1.1-10-01. Under that requirement, an ASIP that supports fuzzy matching "shall return the attribute value … stored in the authentic source".
      • The demo does fuzzy matching (MatchWithVariation, after transliteration and normalization) and returns no value (REQ-85, REQ-113). Read literally, the demo does not meet 10-01. The matrix marks that requirement failing (etsi-ts-119478.yaml#L132-L135).
    • Why it matters: REQ-85 chooses data minimization on the basis of Annex IV. If 10-01 stays, a Dutch ASIP must choose one of two options. It returns the stored value on MatchWithVariation, or it does no fuzzy matching (only Match or NoMatch).
    • Proposal: add this point as a sub-question to Q-29. The choice between the two options belongs to the question whether the ASI returns the value. Send the point to ETSI (E2).

    F-93: Nothing links the citizen who logs in at the QTSP to the citizen who logs in at the source's authorization server¶

    • Status: open

    The QTSP identifies the citizen to whom it issues an attestation, and the authorization server of the source identifies the citizen again for the token. Nothing links these two identifications. A QTSP can therefore issue an attestation with the data of person B to person A, for example when B scans the QR code. The QTSP cannot compare the two itself, because it must not receive the BSN and the token is opaque to it. The proposal is a Dutch check: the QTSP sends its own identification data in the request or to the authorization server for comparison.

    Evidence, impact and proposal
    • Type: open point in the specification
    • Trigger: SL-28, part 7 of the demo epic (demo issue #546, PR #550, PR #552). Work on issuance to the wallet (demo issue #549) showed that the chain has two identifications of the citizen, with no link between them.
    • Observation:
      • eIDAS art. 24(1): a QTSP that issues a QEAA verifies the identity of the person it issues to. Paragraph 1a(a) names the EUDI wallet for that, "directly or by means of a third party". Annex V(c): the QEAA identifies the subject without ambiguity.
      • ETSI TS 119 478 clause 6.1.3.3 (figure 3): only the authorization server identifies the citizen (REQ-AZSP-6.1.3.1-02), and that data goes into the token. The clause does not describe identification by the QTSP. No requirement links the two identifications, and the security requirements of 6.1.3.4 do not link them either.
      • CIR 2025/1569 art. 9(3): the request "shall set out the attributes and the identification data of the subject". In ETSI, the token carries these data. The QTSP does not supply them. Art. 9(5): member states can impose checks that the QTSP "is acting at the request of a legitimate user".
      • Demo state: in demo v0.9.0, the reference QTSP identifies the citizen itself before the verification, with the NL Wallet or test data (demo issue #560). Issuance to the wallet (PR #570) links to the QTSP login only through names, birth date, and nationalities. Demo issue #549 closed without a check between the two logins.
      • On the same device, the wallet returns the browser with a nonce. The authorization server accepts that return only in the browser of the transaction (PR #573). The QR-code route stays available: another phone can scan the code and complete the session.
    • Why it matters: without a link, a QTSP can issue an attestation with the data of person B to person A. This can happen, for example, when B scans the QR code. The QTSP cannot compare the identifications itself, for two reasons. It must not receive the BSN. Also, RFC 6749 and RFC 9068 treat the access token as opaque to the client.
    • Proposal: the Netherlands fixes a check under art. 9(5), in one of two variants:
      1. The QTSP puts the identification data from its own identification in the request, as art. 9(3) says literally, and has them verified too. Match then means that the citizen at the authorization server has the same name and birth date. Since demo issue #560, the QTSP has these data at request time. The check is not built.
      2. The QTSP gives the data to the authorization server (like login_hint), and the server refuses on a mismatch. - Decide in Q-40. Send the gap to ETSI (E6).
    • Related: F-86, F-88, REQ-86, REQ-88, REQ-90, REQ-102, Q-40, E6.

    • Status: open

    F-96: ETSI does not name RFC 9207, so a QTSP that verifies at several ASIPs has no defense against mix-up¶

    • Status: open

    An OAuth client that talks to more than one authorization server needs a defense against mix-up attacks. RFC 9700 prefers the issuer parameter in the authorization response, and FAPI 2.0 requires the server to return it. ETSI only asks to take both into account, and the demo does not return the parameter. A QTSP talks to one authorization server per ASI provider, which is exactly the case where RFC 9700 makes the defense mandatory. The proposal is that a Dutch ASI authorization server returns the issuer parameter and that the QTSP checks it.

    Evidence, impact and proposal
    • Type: open point in the specification
    • Trigger: SL-28, part 8 of the demo epic (PR #574): the item-by-item review of RFC 9700 and the FAPI 2.0 Security Profile.
    • Observation:
      • RFC 9700 §2.1 requires a defense against mix-up for a client that talks to more than one authorization server. The preferred defense is the iss parameter in the authorization response (RFC 9207). FAPI 2.0 §5.3.2.2 requires the server to return iss.
      • ETSI TS 119 478 clause 6.1.3.4 only asks to take both "into account" and does not name RFC 9207. Clause 6.1.3.1 does not require it.
      • The demo returns code and state, without iss. The metadata does not announce authorization_response_iss_parameter_supported (security-considerations.md#L23).
    • Why it matters: a QTSP talks to one authorization server per ASIP. In the EU, that is one server per member state or per source. RFC 9700 makes the defense mandatory for exactly this case. Without iss, the QTSP must tell servers apart with its own redirect URI per server (RFC 9700 §4.4.2). That method is a choice of each QTSP. It is part of the profile that ETSI leaves open (F-84).
    • Proposal: record in the PSA (S11, B-14) that a Dutch ASI authorization server returns iss per RFC 9207, and that a QTSP checks it (Q-66). Send the point to ETSI (E9), so that clause 6.1.3 includes it. The return of iss is not built in demo v0.9.0.
    • Related: F-84, Q-66, E9, ADR-0037.

    F-97: FR-22 to FR-24 name the same statuses with different words, and the rationale of FR-23 contradicts its criteria¶

    • Status: open

    FR-22 to FR-24 of FR-DvTP use different words for the same consent statuses. One rationale uses "Openstaand" (pending) next to "Actief" (active). A revoked consent is "Geannuleerd" (cancelled) in one place and "ingetrokken" (revoked) in another. The rationale of FR-23 allows revocation only while the status is "Open", but its criteria also allow it after retrieval. Builders and reviewers therefore get two readings of one rule. The proposal is one set of terms, with "Ingetrokken" for a revoked consent, and a rationale that matches the criteria.

    Evidence, impact and proposal
    • Type: contradiction in the source (FR-DvTP v0.9)
    • Trigger: a review of the decision points for the build phase, track B (October 27, 2026).
    • Observation:
      • FR-22, criterion 1, names the statuses active ("Actief"), retrieved and still valid ("Opgehaald (nog geldig)"), expired ("Verlopen"), and cancelled ("Geannuleerd"). The rationale of FR-22 uses "Openstaand" for the same status.
      • FR-23 names the action revoke ("Intrekken") and the outcome "Geannuleerd". FR-24 uses "ingetrokken".
      • The rationale of FR-23 says that revocation is possible only when the status is "Open". FR-23, criterion 1, and FR-24, criterion 1, also allow revocation after "Opgehaald", while the consent is valid.
    • Why it matters: builders and reviewers get two readings of one rule. The demo shows "Actief", "Verlopen", and "Ingetrokken" (StatusChip.tsx#L4-L6). "Opgehaald" is missing, because no decision exists on when a consent counts as retrieved (Q-42, F-19).
    • Proposal: use one set of terms in FR-DvTP: "Actief", "Opgehaald (nog geldig)", "Verlopen", and "Ingetrokken". The last term matches the action "Intrekken". Make the FR-23 rationale match the criteria: revocation is possible on "Actief" and "Opgehaald". The choice for the build (October 27, 2026) is "Actief" for the first status and "Ingetrokken" for a revoked consent (REQ-109).
    • Addressee: owner of FR-DvTP.
    • Related: F-19, REQ-38, REQ-109, Q-42, Q-48.

    F-98: FR-DvTP v0.9 contradicts decisions and its own criteria on four points, and PSA S02 is ambiguous on one point¶

    • Status: open

    FR-DvTP v0.9 conflicts with decisions or with its own criteria on four points, and the PSA is ambiguous on one point. FR-39 and FR-26 put policy and a first check at a central place, while the decisions keep PAP and PDP at each bronhouder. FR-25 assumes that revocation ends at retrieval, and FR-35 describes register contents that differ from the chosen design. The PSA calls pseudonymization "transparent" to the citizen, which can mean invisible or explained. The FR-DvTP owner must make four changes and the PSA authors must fix the meaning, so that builders do not get two conflicting sources.

    Evidence, impact and proposal
    • Type: contradiction in the source
    • Trigger: a design round compared FR-DvTP v0.9 and the PSA with the requirements (November 2, 2026).
    • Observation:
      • FR-39 puts the authorization policy at a central policy authority. Its criteria cover centrally managed, sealed bundles (criteria 1 and 2), an RFC process (3), and governance of the authority (4). Criterion 5 puts the mapping of the service catalogue to scopes in that central policy. The decision in demo issue #426 replaced this model (Q-21, closed October 28, 2026). Under that decision, policy management stays at each source, PAP and PDP sit at the bronhouder, and RL-01 to RL-05 stay guidelines. Criterion 5 then belongs in the service description (REQ-121) instead of a central policy.
      • FR-26, dependency 2, says: "the bronhouder receives a request only after the central authorization component (FR-17) did the first check". That conflicts with Q-02 (PEP, PDP, and PAP decentral at the bronhouder) and with REQ-04 and REQ-68: the PDP of the source does the check. The FR-26 criteria themselves are covered.
      • FR-25 applies the pointer to GDPR rights only "when a consent can no longer be cancelled technically (because the data is retrieved)". Its dependency says "only activated when FR-24 is active (revocation no longer possible)". But FR-23 and FR-24, criterion 1, allow revocation after retrieval while the consent is valid (REQ-109). For that reason, REQ-120 shows the pointer with every consent and after every revocation.
      • FR-35, criterion 1, says: "only a party-specific identifier in place of the BSN and the consent proof are recorded". The design chooses otherwise on purpose (REQ-69, consent register data model, route D in ADR-0040). The register keeps the polymorphic PI and PP under its own reference. It keeps no party-specific values, and it signs the proof without keeping it. Both approaches keep no BSN, but they record different data.
      • PSA S02 says: "pseudonymization and technical identifiers are transparent to the citizen. The interface does not present them as a choice of content." The second part is clear and is in REQ-117. "Transparent" can mean two things: the citizen sees nothing of it, or the interface explains it to the citizen (PSA P-13).
    • Why it matters: the requirements follow the decisions (Q-02, Q-21) and the chosen design (route D). If the FR-DvTP text stays, builders and reviewers get two conflicting sources. The S02 UI requirement stays incomplete until the meaning of "transparent" is fixed.
    • Proposal:
      • The owner of FR-DvTP must make four changes:
        • Rewrite FR-39 to policy at the bronhouder, with the catalogue-to-set mapping in the service description.
        • Make FR-26 dependency 2 refer to the PDP of the source instead of a central authorization component.
        • Detach FR-25 from "revocation no longer possible". Apply it to every consent and after a revocation.
        • Make FR-35, criterion 1, describe what the register really keeps: its own reference with PI and PP, no party-specific values, and not the proof.
      • The PSA authors must fix in S02 whether "transparent" means invisible or explained.
    • Addressee: owner of FR-DvTP, and the PSA authors (S02).
    • Related: F-06, F-09, F-97, REQ-04, REQ-68, REQ-69, REQ-109, REQ-117, REQ-120, REQ-121, RL-01, RL-05, Q-02, Q-21, ADR-0021, ADR-0040.

    Summary for the PSA¶

    The demo shows that the generic chain works end to end for three routes: DvTP, EUDI issuance, and the Authentic Source Interface (ASI). The following sections group the open findings per theme (state of November 2, 2026) and name what the PSA must decide.

    An earlier version of this summary named six open design knots. All six are closed: F-02, F-03, F-04, F-06, F-07, and F-08. The requirements and ADRs cover PDP placement, consent atomicity, the consent lifecycle, the fourth validation point, LDV, and mTLS on FSC.

    In total, 34 findings are open. The review of September 21, 2026 closed 28 of 39 findings and kept 11 open. After that review, 24 findings were added, and the demo resolves one of them. F-54 is open again, and ADR-0021 closed F-09.

    Policy management¶

    • Closed: F-09 (signing only across an organization boundary, ADR-0021).
    • Open: F-51 (management endpoints behind the decision policy), F-52 (cumulative store), and F-98 (FR-39 still describes a central policy authority).
    • Decided: policy management stays at each source. PAP and PDP sit at the bronhouder (Q-21). RL-01 to RL-05 are guidelines for bronhouders.
    • PSA to decide: whether bundle signing is required when a bundle crosses an organization boundary (ADR-0021).

    Consent and its lifecycle¶

    • Open: F-19 (no "retrieved" status), F-74 (scope is the intersection of legal ceiling and source offer), F-78 (the proof freezes at consent), and F-97 and F-98 (FR-DvTP contradictions).
    • Decided: the legal basis travels as a signed proof. The status is checked online and fails closed (F-61, ADR-0032). An integrator connects under FSC delegation, and the consent binds the represented party (F-73, ADR-0034).
    • PSA to decide:
      1. When a consent counts as retrieved, and how the register learns it (Q-42).
      2. A DvTP catalogue per FR-32 as the single source for scopes, with an intersection check at activation (B-03, REQ-121).
      3. A version for the proof, and how long an old version stays valid (REQ-82).
      4. What "transparent" means in S02 (F-98).

    Authorization regime¶

    • Open: F-70. The regime follows from the proof (ADR-0031), but only consent is positive proof. The PID regime rests on an actor list and an unverified BSN.
    • PSA to decide: what the evidence for the EUDI legal basis is (REQ-45, Q-18). The demo resolves the binding of rules to services (F-90), and this binding is a proposal for S05.

    Pseudonymization and BSNk¶

    • Open: F-75 (the consent facility must be a recognized BSNk party), F-76 (the consumer has no citizen identifier), F-77 (one subject per request), and F-79 (only the Inway reaches the source gateway, with the roll-out outside Compose and the nonce open).
    • Decided in the TO: the source gateway decrypts its own value after the allow (ADR-0033). Route D for citizen recognition is proposed (ADR-0040).
    • PSA to decide:
      1. Recognition of the consent facility at BSNk, and who the BSNk participant is (Q-26, Q-49).
      2. Whether the consumer gets a pseudonym (VP), with its effects on logging, access, and recognition (Q-01).
      3. Queries about more than one person.

    Admission and service descriptions¶

    • Open: F-57 (the source owns its description, but the DCAT-AP-NL reference and central findability are missing), and F-71 (an operator takes over a description, and the recorded agreement of the bronhouder is not built).
    • Decided: admission is a register entry instead of a policy rule (F-55).
    • PSA to decide: accept or reject the role split "the source publishes, GBO validates and activates" (S10), and decide where central findability lives.

    Audit and logging¶

    • Open: F-54 (BSN in both decision logs on the EUDI and ASI routes), F-68 (no route from the ADL at the source to the consent logbook), F-72 (the ADL lacks policy version, consent status answer, and configuration), and F-76 (what the consumer logs under).
    • Decided: three standards give three records. ADL is for decisions (ADR-0036), LDV is per controller (ADR-0026), and FSC-Logging is for transport. W3C Trace Context is the correlation key (ADR-0035). The register index covers the chain start (F-68 point 1).
    • PSA to decide: keep identifiers out of the PDP input instead of masking logs (ADR-0043, proposed). The questions to Logius (Q-25, Q-27) and to OpenFTV (fill the ADL fields) need an owner.

    EUDI issuance¶

    Authentic Source Interface¶

    • Open: F-80, F-84 to F-89, and F-91 to F-96.
