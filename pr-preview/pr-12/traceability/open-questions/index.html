    Open questions¶

    An open question is a question that the demo cannot answer. It needs a decision by the PSA, by a system forum (G5, Logius, the standards bodies), or a legal assessment. Each entry names the forum, the options, and the working assumption. The demo and this TO use the working assumption until the forum decides the question. An architecture decision that waits for a question has the status proposed in the decision log.

    A question has one of four status values: open, partly answered, answered, or closed. The register never reuses a question number. A renumbered question keeps a history line with its old number.

    Q-01: Which pseudonym form does the system use?¶

    • Status: partly answered

    PSA P-04 asks that a consumer without a statutory power gets a citizen identity that nobody can link outside its context. It is a legal question whether a private consumer without a public task can connect to BSNk as a recipient. It is also open whether an encrypted identity that the consumer cannot decrypt counts as a processing of the BSN. The answer decides how the consumer logs the citizen, gives access, and recognizes one citizen across consents. The working assumption is that the consumer gets no pseudonym, and the consent proof carries an encrypted value per source that each source decrypts itself.

    Options, working assumption and links
    • Forum: PSA B-07 (pseudonymous identity). Sub-question 1: Logius (BSNk management organization) and the Ministry of the Interior and Kingdom Relations (BZK). Sub-question 2: lawyer and BZK.
    • Question: PSA and GO set the direction of the form. PSA P-04 says that a consumer without a statutory power gets "a party-specific identity or pseudonym" that cannot be linked outside its context. The GO says the same for the private service provider (dienstverlener). Two sub-questions stay open:
      1. Is a private consumer without a public task allowed to connect to BSNk PP as a recipient? If so, on which legal basis (grondslag)?
      2. The consumer carries an encrypted identity for the source that the consumer cannot decrypt. Is that a processing of the BSN by the consumer?
    • Answer so far:
      • At BSNk, the recipient is the party (OIN). The service is not the recipient.
      • The consent proof carries an encrypted value per party. Each source decrypts its own value locally (REQ-21, REQ-80).
      • The BSNk technical specifications v11.2 agree. The key belongs to the OIN and not to the FSC peer. Two peers with the same OIN share one encrypted value and one key.
      • A pseudonym per service needs a separate OIN (BSNK-07).
    • Why the demo cannot answer it: Both sub-questions are legal questions under the BSN General Provisions Act (Wabb) and the GDPR. Technically, BSNk can do it. Every service provider with a PKIoverheid certificate gets keys for pseudonyms. BSNk also supports an OIN derived from a Chamber of Commerce (KvK) number (specification p. 23, p. 67). But the specification is written for service providers with a public task (p. 26). In the reading of the GBO team, a consumer without a public task falls outside it.
    • Options:
      • (a) No pseudonym for the consumer. The consumer then has no citizen identifier at all. This option affects three things:
        • the identifier that the consumer logs under (Q-27),
        • the access of a citizen at the consumer,
        • the recognition of a citizen across consents (F-76).
      • (b) An encrypted pseudonym (VP) from BSNk for the consumer. This option needs a yes on sub-question 1.
    • Current working assumption: The working assumption is option a:
      • The demo gives the consumer no pseudonym (PR #469).
      • Since PR #477, the proof carries a VP for every source. For a source on the BSN Authorization List, it also carries an encrypted identity (VI). Thus sub-question 2 is already the practice of the demo.
      • Issue #478 tracks a pseudonym for the consumer. Issue #377 tracks a test against the real BSNk PP.
      • A point for the choice of form: with a pseudonym per recipient, the LDV dataSubjectId differs per controller (verantwoordelijke). Chain-wide citizen access can then search only by trace ID, from the own organization (F-65). This is a privacy property and not a defect.
    • Related: F-65, F-75, F-76, REQ-20, REQ-21, REQ-80, BSNK-07, Q-26 (recognition of the consent facility (toestemmingsvoorziening) at BSNk), Q-27, ADR 0005, ADR 0033.

    Q-08: What belongs in the LDV (processing log), and who holds the retention duty?¶

    • Status: open

    GBO builds and runs three components: the consent register (toestemmingsregister), the EUDI adapter, and the issuance server. Each component records its own processings in an LDV logbook, and the PDP records every decision in the ADL. Nobody decided how long these records stay, or who can read them. That is a legal and supervision decision, and retention is harder because an ADL record needs its sources to stay available. In the working assumption, the demo keeps separate stores and a read-only role for the ADL, without a retention policy or tamper evidence.

    Options, working assumption and links
    • Forum: management organization and privacy officer. PSA S09 ("agreements set retention, access, inspection and destruction explicitly"), B-10.
    • Question: GBO builds and operates three components: the consent register, the eudi-adapter, and the issuance server. Which retention periods apply to the LDV and ADL records of these components? And who can read those records?
    • Answer so far:
      • The field schema is LDV plus ADL (PSA S09).
      • The ADL is the authoritative decision log (PR #373). The policy version goes in adl.core.policies (REQ-59).
      • Logius keeps three related logging standards with one trace ID: LDV, ADL, and FSC logging (F-63).
      • Each component records the events of its own responsibility, decentrally. This also applies on the wallet side (S09, B-10).
      • The citizen access model and central audit aggregation belong to B-10 and the open decisions of S09. This question does not cover them.
    • Why the demo cannot answer it: Retention periods and read access are a legal and supervision decision. Two points make retention harder:
      • The ADL source-reference model requires that a source stays available as long as a record that points to it exists.
      • LDV deliberately sets no access model for its read API. The access-control module of the API strategy is a candidate.
    • Options: None named. The management organization and the privacy officer give the periods and the access model as input.
    • Current working assumption:
      • The demo has separate stores and a SELECT-only reader role for the ADL.
      • The demo has no retention policy, no WORM storage, and no tamper evidence for ADL, LDV, or FSC transaction logs (REQ-32).
      • The LDV field processing_activity_id needs a register of processing activities. In the demo, this register is a stand-in per logbook, without legal status. The real register is a dependency on the management organization (REQ-72).
      • Since PR #370, the demo deviates on purpose from the S09 minimization norm (REQ-60).
    • Related: F-53, F-54, F-63, F-72, REQ-32, REQ-55, REQ-59, REQ-60, REQ-71, REQ-72, Q-25, Q-27, ADR 0026, ADR 0036.

    Q-13: Who sets the validity period of a consent?¶

    • Status: open

    The functional requirements give each service a default validity period from the service catalog and let the citizen adjust it within bounds. It is open whether the citizen really must be able to change the period, and within which bounds. The choice is a policy and UX question under the GDPR, which the demo can show but cannot decide. The demo deviates from the requirements: the consumer sends the end date, and the citizen cannot change the period. Without an end date, the consent register uses one year.

    Options, working assumption and links
    • Forum: FR-DvTP owner, management organization (defaults and bounds in the service catalog), PSA B-06 (consent in DvTP).
      • FR-11 (FR-DvTP v0.9) says that the default period per service comes from the service catalog (FR-32). It also says that the citizen can adjust the period within the permitted bounds.
      • FR-05, criterion 5, says the same for the screen. Thus FR-05 and FR-11 agree.
      • It is open whether the citizen really must be able to adjust the period. The PSA capabilities only say that the portal shows the period to the citizen.

      Question: Reopened on November 2, 2026.

      Must the citizen be able to change the default validity period of a service? If so, within which bounds? The question which component enforces those bounds is Q-62, sub-question 1.

    • Why the demo cannot answer it: It is a policy and UX choice (GDPR art. 7). The demo can show each variant. It cannot decide which variant is lawful and wanted.

    • Options:
      • (a) A fixed default per service from the catalog. The citizen sees the period and does not change it.
      • (b) The citizen adjusts the period within bounds from the catalog, as the text of FR-11 says.
      • (c) The consumer sets the period in the request, within the catalog ceiling. No source names the consumer for this role. The demo does this in v0.9.0.
      • The consumer sends valid_until in the redirect context (REQ-37).
      • The portal turns this value into validity_seconds (PortaalConsent.tsx#L85).
      • If there is no value, the register uses one year (consent-register/main.go#L51).
      • The citizen cannot change the period.

      Current working assumption: No requirement says that the citizen adjusts the period (REQ-19, REQ-117, register data model). The demo deviates from FR-11:

      Issue #380 tracks the deviation. The issue also covers the adjust option that the demo removed earlier.

    • Related: FR-05, FR-11, FR-32, F-11, REQ-19, REQ-37, REQ-117, Q-04, Q-62, Q-76 (per consent or per source).

    Q-14: Query enforcement model: how does the PDP model fields, recursion, and arguments?¶

    • Status: open

    The PSA speaks of pre-registered, selective data requests, and the GO says that the service catalog holds the permitted requests per application. The question is whether queries are fixed per application, or whether a consumer can write its own queries within a schema that the source publishes. The answer decides how rich the catalog gets and how close GBO stays to iWlz (the data exchange of the Dutch Long-term Care Act). The working assumption rejects fixed queries for DvTP, so the consumer writes its own query and the PDP decides every field against an allowlist. If the field list is uncertain, the PDP denies the request.

    Options, working assumption and links
    • Forum: PSA and GO authors, through B-02 (API profile) and B-03 (service catalog). The technical enforcement model belongs in the GraphQL profile proposal for OpenFTV (Q-16, issue #331).
    • Question: PSA S07 says that the API supports "pre-registered, selective data requests". The GO says that the service catalog "contains the permitted data requests per application". Are the queries then fixed per application? Or is a different model sufficient, as follows:
      • The source publishes a (partial) schema per service.
      • The consumer writes its own queries within that schema.
      • The PDP validates each query against the schema (fields, nesting, arguments).
    • Why the demo cannot answer it: The demo can show each model. The meaning of S07 is for its authors to say. Three points are design and maintainability choices: which model applies per regime, how rich the catalog gets, and how close GBO stays to iWlz.
    • Options:
      • (A) The consumer picks one of a list of fixed query templates (iWlz, historically).
      • (B) The consumer writes its own query. The PDP parses it and checks each field against an allowlist (the demo).
      • (C) Schema directives say per field which policies run. A PEP preprocessor parses the query once and gives the PDP structured input (iWlz, present practice).
      • (D) The catalog fixes a maximum template or schema per scope. The query of the consumer must be a structural subset of it.
      • A middle form (findings review, September 21, 2026): the source publishes a (partial) schema with filter arguments. Per application, policy requires which paths are mandatory, and which filters with which fixed values. Policy does not look at content. The queries are then free, but the bounds for them are fixed. This form fits the GO and the iWlz network model. Issue #425 tests it for an attestation about another person (REQ-57).
    • Current working assumption: The team does not want fixed queries for DvTP. The DvTP path uses model B. The PDP side builds the complete field list per the FTV GraphQL profile and decides every field separately. If the field list is uncertain, the PDP denies the request (REQ-17). Since SL-13, consent works at scope level, and that makes D a small next step. SL-14 built a proof of concept for D.
    • Related: REQ-17, REQ-57, F-74, Q-16, SL-03a, SL-03b, ADR 0001, ADR 0012.

    Q-17: EUDI issuance architecture: who owns the issuance server and the adapter?¶

    • Status: open

    GBO offers a central, optional facility that issues attestations for the EUDI Wallet, and each bronhouder stays responsible for its attestations. Can the facility hold the seal key of a bronhouder, or must it call a signing facility under control of the bronhouder? It is also open who holds the qualified seal certificate, which names the authentic source, and the status-list certificate. Key custody and certificate holding are legal and governance decisions. As a working assumption, the demo uses one issuance server, a certificate set per source, and keys in files, which decides nothing for production.

    Options, working assumption and links
    • Forum: BZK, the Dutch Authority for Digital Infrastructure (RDI), and bronhouders, with a legal review. PSA B-13 and the open decisions of PSA S11, issue #225.
    • Question: The question became a key and seal question, with three parts:
      1. Can a central PuB-EAA facility hold and use the seal key material of a bronhouder? Or must sealing go through a signing facility that the bronhouder manages (remote signing, an HSM at the source)?
      2. Who holds the qualified seal certificate?
      3. Is the status-list certificate of the source or of the facility?
    • Answer so far:
      • The choice "one issuance server per bronhouder or one central server" is obsolete.
      • The GO gives GBO the technical role of a central, optional issuance facility: "GBO is not itself a PubEAA provider".
      • In PSA S11, the bronhouder stays legally and materially responsible.
      • The source publishes its own attestation schema and offer (F-57, REQ-64).
      • Issuer and reader certificates are a set per source. If material is missing, issuance fails hard (REQ-58).
      • Onboarding is REQ-65 and B-09.
      • The regime follows from the evidence and not from a flow field (REQ-74).
    • Why the demo cannot answer it: Key custody and the holder of a qualified certificate are legal and governance decisions. The regulation makes them concrete:
      • Attestations: the seal on a PuB-EAA is created in a QSCD (ETSI TS 119 471, REQ-EAASP-7.5.3-02, through CIR 2026/1735). The seal uses a qualified certificate per ETSI TS 119 412-6 clause 8. That certificate names the authentic source and the legal basis (eIDAS art. 45f(1)(b)).
      • ASI results: from January 1, 2027, the public sector body or the designated intermediary seals them. It uses at least an advanced seal on a qualified seal certificate. A QSCD is not required there (CIR 2025/1569 art. 9(4), as amended by 2026/1735).
    • Options:
      • (a) The central facility holds the key of each bronhouder.
      • (b) Remote signing or an HSM under control of the bronhouder, which the facility calls.
    • Current working assumption: One issuance server with a certificate set per source. The demo keeps the keys in files at the facility. This choice decides nothing about production.
    • Related: F-66, F-80, F-81, REQ-58, Q-07, Q-28, Q-32, Q-37, ADR 0028, ADR 0041.

    Q-18: EUDI flow security: phishing, certificate management, data processing in the adapter, PID verification¶

    • Status: open

    Can a bronhouder identify a citizen with a BSN from a verified EUDI Wallet PID, with the legal status of a BSN from DigiD? If so, the chain must show how the verified disclosure binds to the source query. This is a national legal question, because eIDAS does not name the BSN and the member state decides on the number. In the working assumption, the issuance server verifies the PID but passes only plain attributes to the adapter, without an authenticated callback. So nothing in the chain binds the BSN of the source query to a verified PID.

    Options, working assumption and links
    • Forum: lawyer and BZK. PSA B-08 (identity matching), PSA S11.
    • Question: The question is about a BSN from a verified PID disclosure (EUDI Wallet). Can a bronhouder use that BSN to identify the citizen in a source query, with the same legal status as a BSN from DigiD? If so, which binding between the verified disclosure and the query must the chain show (issue #133)?
    • Answer so far: The original question was split.
      • Presentation and combination of attestations by verifiers are outside GBO (GO pattern A).
      • The designation of the Dutch PID issuer is an external dependency.
      • The issuance server verifies the PID signature.
      • The security basis for the build covers phishing, certificate rotation and compromise, and trust-list use. It also covers data processing and logging in the adapter, state, and data freshness. See REQ-77 and the checklist in issue #381.
    • Why the demo cannot answer it: It is a national legal question.
      • eIDAS does not name the BSN. It requires that the PID "uniquely represents" the person (art. 5a(5)(f)). It forbids use that can be "tracked, linked or correlated" (art. 5a(16)(a)).
      • personal_administrative_number stays optional. The member state sets its policy in its eID scheme (CIR 2024/2977). CIR 2026/1731 did not change this.
      • Without a number, identity matching per CIR 2025/846 applies.
      • (a) The issuance server authenticates its callback to the adapter (mTLS or a signed request).
      • (b) The adapter verifies the SD-JWT disclosure itself.

      Options: For the legal part, the answer is yes or no. For the binding (REQ-45), there are two options:

      In both cases, the disclosure is bound to the request, and the PID trust list holds PID issuers only.

    • Current working assumption: The issuance server verifies the wallet presentation, but gives the adapter only plain attributes. The adapter does not authenticate that callback. Nothing in the chain binds the BSN of the source query to a verified PID disclosure. If a request carries no consent proof, it gets the PID regime (REQ-74, F-70). SL-25 (PID verification in the adapter) is a dropped demo increment.

    • Related: F-70, REQ-45, REQ-74, REQ-77, Q-15, Q-36 (PID without BSN), ADR 0018, ADR 0031.

    Q-25: How can a reader find the step from the decision at the source to the consent log?¶

    • Status: open

    If the PDP of a source checks a consent status, the consent register logs that check in its own LDV logbook. The PDP writes only an ADL decision record, and the ADL has no field that points to another LDV logbook. A reader who follows the standard links therefore cannot get from the decision to the consent log. The question is how the ADL record can point there, and the answer needs Logius or a design choice. The working assumption accepts the gap: an auditor finds the register record by a search on the trace ID.

    Options, working assumption and links
    • Forum: Logius (maintainers of ADL and LDV), PSA S09.
      • The caller is the PDP of the source. The PDP writes an ADL record and no LDV record (F-63).
      • Thus no LDV record at the source points to the consent logbook with nextLogbookId. The ADL has no equivalent. A reader who follows the standard route does not get to the consent logbook (F-68, point 2).
      • ADL 1.0.0 gives adl.core.information a reference per information source (PIP). That reference must be "sufficient to retrieve, on demand, the data … that affected the authorization decision".
      • That field is for rebuilding the input of the decision, in a free format. It does not mention LDV or nextLogbookId.

    Q-33: Does the Netherlands impose per-attribute authorization for ASI requests under art. 9(5)?¶

    • Status: open

    Under the ETSI standard, a QTSP gets a token per operation, and that token is valid for every attribute while it lasts. The implementing regulation lets a member state add controls, such as a binding of the token to the attributes that the citizen came for. Such a binding limits what a QTSP can request, but a QTSP that implements only ETSI fails on it. The question is whether the Netherlands imposes this authorization per attribute. In the working assumption, the binding is optional on request of the QTSP, and a setting in the demo makes it mandatory.

    Options, working assumption and links
    • Forum: BZK, and a lawyer or DPO.

    • Question: ETSI TS 119 478 authorizes an ASI request per operation, with the scopes verify and retrieve (clause 6.1.3.2, figure 2). The QTSP puts the attributes to verify in the body of the request ("shall set out the attributes", art. 9(3)). The token does not hold them. So a token with scope verify is valid for every attribute while the token is valid.

      • For sub-question 2: (a) fetch the missing data from the BRP, or (b) the BSN alone is sufficient.

    Q-37: What is GBO's role in the ASI: designated intermediary, technical supplier to bronhouders, or authentic source itself?¶

    • Status: open

    GBO builds one ASIP for several authentic sources, and the law allows an ASIP to be the source itself or a designated intermediary. The question is whether GBO becomes that designated intermediary, stays a technical supplier to each bronhouder, or becomes an authentic source itself. The role decides who seals the result and who answers for the correctness of the data. The working assumption is the intermediary model, which the GBO team saw as likely on October 14, 2026. The demo names an authentic source for every data service and uses one seal for all of them.

    Options, working assumption and links
    • Forum: BZK (system responsibility), the bronhouders, and a lawyer or DPO.

    • Question: GBO builds one ASIP for more than one authentic source (F-89). The sources describe the role of an ASIP in these ways:

      • ETSI TS 119 478 says that the ASIP "can be an authentic source or an intermediary acting on its behalf" (clause 4.2).
      • CIR 2025/1569 art. 9(2) names "authentic sources or designated intermediaries recognized at national level".
      • Art. 9(4) lets the result name the source "or, where applicable, the public sector body designated to act on behalf of the authentic source".
      • 2026/1735 lets the source "or the designated intermediary" seal the result.
      • eIDAS art. 3(47) defines an authentic source as a system that "contains and provides" the data. That system is a primary source "or recognized as authentic in accordance with Union or national law".

      Does GBO (or the management organization) become the designated intermediary for ASI verification on behalf of the bronhouders? Or does each bronhouder stay ASIP itself, with GBO as technical supplier? Or is GBO itself recognized as an authentic source? In each case, who seals, and who answers for the correctness of the data?

    • Why the demo cannot answer it: The role follows from a designation and from the statutory responsibility for correctness. Those are administrative and legal decisions.

    • Options:
      • (X) GBO is the designated intermediary. provider is GBO, authenticSource is the bronhouder. GBO seals with one qualified seal of its own. This needs a national designation. The responsibility for correctness stays with the bronhouder.
      • (Y) Each bronhouder is ASIP itself, and GBO supplies the technology. provider is the bronhouder, there is no authenticSource, and each bronhouder seals itself. GBO is processor or technical service provider. GBO can possibly run one hosted service that has an identity and a seal per source.
      • (Z) GBO is itself the authentic source. provider is GBO, and there is no authenticSource. This needs statutory recognition of the GBO system as authentic. GBO then answers for data it does not manage, next to the base register that the law designates.

    • Current working assumption: Model X. In the team estimate of October 14, 2026, model X is likely and model Z is not. The demo has provider "GBO demo ASIP" (dataservices.json#L6). In its source description, the demo names the BRP as authentic source by its legal name. The demo uses one seal.

      The provider is set per data service, but a seal per data service is not built. Since issue #592 step 5, every data service names an authentic source. So the demo does not allow a data service with the ASIP itself as source.

    • Related: F-80, F-89, REQ-94, E3, Q-17 (who holds the seal), Q-28 (who the Netherlands designates), Q-32 (seal format), ADR 0037, ADR 0038.

    Q-70: Which normalization applies in fuzzy matching per kind of value, and who sets it?¶

    • Status: open

    The ASI allows fuzzy matching only for free text, to accept spelling variations, and excludes dates, codes and numbers. The demo normalizes white space, case, hyphens and diacritics, but not every kind of value tolerates all of these rules. The question is whether the rules differ per kind of value, and who sets that kind: the ASIP, the source or the EU catalogue. In the demo, every string without a format gets the full normalization. Postal code and country code have no format, so they get it too, and that conflicts with the requirement.

    Options, working assumption and links
    • Forum: tech lead, PSA/GO authors (ASI profile).

    • Question: REQ-113 limits fuzzy matching to free text. It excludes dates, codes, and numbers. ETSI TS 119 478 REQ-ASIP-6.1.1.1-10 names "orthographic variations" and leaves the rules free. The demo normalization has three rules: equal white space and case, equal hyphens, and removal of diacritics with transliteration per ICAO 9303.

      Not every kind of value tolerates all three rules. For a name, all three rules fit. A postal code that differs only in a space or in case (1234AB and 1234 ab) is clearly the same value. For a country code, case folding is the most that fits. The question has three parts: 1. Does the normalization apply per kind of value? Which rules belong to postal codes and to codes? 2.     • Options: The normalization can follow one of four rule sets:
      • (a) One normalization for every string without format.
      • (b) Normalization per kind of value. Names get the full normalization. Postal codes get only the white space and case rule. Codes get no normalization, or only the case rule. The ASIP sets the kind per attribute identifier.
      • (c) As option b, but the source gives the kind in value_schema, for example with format.
      • (d) The rules that the EU catalogue of attributes sets per attribute. This option needs the catalogue to exist (Q-38).

    • Current working assumption: Option a. Since demo issue #592 steps 1 to 5 (PR #624), the source describes its attributes. The ASIP takes the rule from value_schema.

      A string with a format matches exactly, for example the birth date (format: date). Every string without a format gets the full normalization (match.go#L31-L49, match.go#L137-L158). Postal code and country code have no format (asi-attributes.json#L44-L55). As a result, they also get the full normalization. This conflicts with REQ-113.

    Q-78: How does the reference policy show that it is not tied to one policy language or engine?¶

    • Status: open

    The PSA requires that policy is product-independent and not tied to one policy language or engine. The reference policy of GBO uses one language for one engine. A source with another PDP cannot take it over or show equal decisions. The question is how GBO shows independence: rules as data, a shared decision test set, or one open language with an exit path. There is no decision, and the demo has one engine and one policy language.

    Options, working assumption and links
    • Forum: tech lead, PSA S05, S06.

    • Question: Which option does GBO follow for its reference policy? Does the requirement also apply to policy that a bronhouder writes itself, or only as a guideline (RL)? PSA S05 requires that "the interface and the policy are product-independent". S06 requires that policy is "not tied to one specific policy language or implementation" (see also P-09). REQ-42 covers the interface.

      The reference policy that GBO delivers is in one policy language, for one engine. A source that chooses another PDP cannot take this policy over. That source also cannot show that its decisions are the same.

    • Why the demo cannot answer it: The demo runs one engine. Only a second engine can show whether the policy is portable.

    • Options: GBO can show the independence in one of three ways:
      • (a) The content of the rules (service, actor, sets, fields, regime) as data, apart from the engine code, with a thin layer per engine.
      • (b) A decision test set apart from the engine, which every PDP must pass, next to the policy in one language. Each test maps an AuthZEN request to the expected decision and reason.
      • (c) Accept one open policy language as sufficient, with a recorded exit path (D-13).
    • Current working assumption: No decision. The demo has one engine and one policy language.
    • Related: REQ-42, RL-01, ADR 0021, Q-16, Q-21.

    Q-79: Does GBO build the OOTS adapter, or does the OOTS-V operator extend its facility with the GBO interface?¶

    • Status: open

    The PSA lets an OOTS adapter connect to the national interface of OOTS-V, and OOTS requests use the same authorization and logging as other requests. The question is whether GBO builds that adapter, or the OOTS-V operator extends its facility with the GBO interface. The choice decides the route to the source, the regime and evidence for OOTS, and the versioned mapping to OOTS-EDM. The PDP knows only the consent and PID regimes, so it denies an OOTS request without its own evidence. There is no working assumption, because the demo has no OOTS route.

    Options, working assumption and links
    • Forum: BZK and OOTS coordination, tech lead, PSA B-12, S08, S10.

    • Question: Does GBO build the OOTS adapter, or does the OOTS-V operator extend its facility with the GBO interface? Where is the boundary between OOTS-V and the mapping function?

      PSA S08 lets the OOTS adapter connect to the national interface of OOTS-V. OOTS requests use the same generic authorization and logging as other data requests. Bronhouders do not depend on AS4/eDelivery. The Basisinrichting OOTS stays responsible for transport, discovery, and the European process interaction. If the bronhouder configures a mapping, a central mapping function translates to OOTS-EDM.

      Who builds the adapter is an open decision of S08. This design has no OOTS requirement. Three items depend on the choice: - The route of an OOTS request to the source. - The regime and the evidence for OOTS. REQ-74 knows only the consent regime and the PID regime. As a result, the PDP denies an OOTS request without its own evidence. - The mapping to OOTS-EDM with traceable versions, as REQ-124 requires for issuance.

    • Why the demo cannot answer it: The demo does not touch OOTS.

    • Options: The OOTS adapter can come from one of two parties:
      • (a) GBO builds the OOTS adapter, behind the national interface of OOTS-V.
      • (b) The OOTS-V operator extends its facility with the GBO interface. GBO delivers only the generic source access.
    • Current working assumption: None. The demo has no OOTS route.

    Q-83: Which authorization server identifies the citizen when an attestation is issued with the authorization code flow?¶

    • Status: open

    HAIP 1.0 requires the authorization code flow for issuance. In that flow, an authorization server identifies the citizen and records consent, instead of the citizen sharing the PID with the issuer. OpenID4VCI 1.0 leaves open how that server identifies the citizen. ETSI TS 119 478 covers only verification for QTSPs. GBO already plans an authorization server for the ASI that identifies the citizen and shows a request page per source. The question is which authorization server identifies the citizen for issuance, and what that means for the requirements that assume only a PID disclosure.

    Options, working assumption and links
    • Forum: tech lead. PSA S11.

    • Question: HAIP 1.0 section 4 says that the wallet and the credential issuer must support the authorization code flow. OpenID4VCI 1.0 section 3 leaves the authentication of the citizen to the authorization server. One example the specification gives is a credential presentation.

      1. Which authorization server identifies the citizen and records consent for issuance with the authorization code flow?
      2. If the authorization server of the ASI also serves issuance: which clients and resources does it accept? REQ-88 and REQ-89 admit only clients with the entitlement QEAA_Provider.
      3. REQ-45 takes the subject of a source query only from a verified PID disclosure. Which identification replaces it in this flow, and which identification means count (Q-36)?

    • Why the demo cannot answer it: It is a choice about components and responsibilities: which party identifies the citizen and records consent for issuance. The demo can build each option, but cannot choose between them.

    • Options:
      • (a) The issuer has its own authorization server, with its own login.
      • (b) The authorization server of the ASI is also the authorization server of the issuer. The wallet sends its authorization request and token request there.
      • (c) The issuer keeps its own authorization server toward the wallet, and is an OAuth client of the authorization server of the ASI. The citizen logs in and consents there. This is the proposal: one place where the citizen logs in and consents per source, in the same pattern as ETSI TS 119 478.
    • Current working assumption: None. The TO describes issuance only after a PID disclosure (ADR 0018).
