    Identity and trust¶

    GBO must know which organization it deals with, and which certificate the chain uses for which purpose. The design keeps two questions apart:

    • Who is this organization? This is the legal identity (OIN and name) and the technical identity on FSC.
    • Which certificates does EUDI need? These are the trust roles of attestation issuance and of the Authentic Source Interface.

    The capabilities are S04 and F1, under P-07 (trust through recognized admission and trust anchors). These decisions apply:

    • ADR-0003: FSC for all connectivity.
    • ADR-0023: part A, organization identity (accepted), and part B, EUDI certificate roles (proposed).
    • ADR-0028: PuB-EAA productization (proposed).

    For the requirements, see Identity, trust and transport.

    Authorization per request is a separate layer. FSC establishes the organization, but this tells nothing about what the organization can do in one request. For more information, see Authorization.

    Three identity carriers¶

    An organization in the chain has three identifiers (F-60):

    Carrier What it identifies Source
    FSC Peer ID the FSC peer: the system that sends or receives FSC traffic subject.serialNumber of the FSC certificate
    OIN the legal organization (bronhouder, consumer) serialNumber of the organization's certificate
    Organization name the legal name that a wallet or a record shows exactly one O attribute of the same certificate

    If the bronhouder runs its own peer, its Peer ID is its OIN. In other cases, the two values can differ. One peer can offer services of more than one bronhouder. Simulation Peer IDs are not OINs, for example 0000009950HYPBV00000. Until SL-49 (PR #242), the demo used one field for both values. In demo v0.9.0, the source configuration keeps provider_peer_id as a separate field (source_configuration.go#L35-L44).

    It is open whether policy can read the Peer ID of a consumer as its OIN. The alternative is that policy takes that link from the admission register (toelatingsregister) (Q-68).

    The legal identity comes from the certificate¶

    A GBO component can put the OIN or name of a bronhouder in an attestation or a decision record. The component takes that value from verified certificate material. A value that an operator can fill in freely is never the source (REQ-67, ADR-0023, part A).

    The certificate content decides the result:

    Certificate Result
    20-digit OIN in serialNumber, exactly one organization name The OIN and the name come from the certificate
    The configured OIN or name differs from the certificate Refused: the component does not load the set
    No 20-digit OIN, or not exactly one organization name Refused (CERTIFICATE_SET_INVALID)

    The rule is in development_ca_provider.go#L280-L288 and source-registry/onboarding/service.go#L293-L297. The source document also names its source_oin. This value must equal the OIN in the certificate set. If it differs, the onboarding refuses the document (Source onboarding). The issuer name, the description, and the logo that the wallet shows also come from the certificate (PR #321).

    This rule has these consequences:

    • The runtime gets leaf material and public CA certificates, but never CA private keys. Provisioning is a separate privileged command.
    • Consents are bound to the identity of the consumer. If that identity changes, the consents become unusable. The migration in PR #244 and PR #277 showed this.
    • In the default demo onboarding, the Belastingdienst peer publishes RvIG with the Belastingdienst OIN. This is a demo shortcut, and it does not show one OIN per bronhouder. The certificate sets still differ per source.

    Every channel goes through FSC¶

    Every channel that calls a source for an external party goes through FSC, with a valid certificate (REQ-46, ADR-0003). DvTP, EUDI issuance, and the ASIP each call through their own Outway. FSC traffic is mTLS between peers. The Manager of the provider issues the access token with the grant hash of the contract.

    FSC refuses a connection with an expired or revoked certificate. open-fsc checks revocation with a CRL cache. An operator can switch off this cache per Inway, Outway, and Manager. The requirement forbids this. The demo does not check this behavior. It is open within which time a revocation must take effect (Q-53).

    Private parties¶

    A private consumer authenticates in two ways, with separate roles (Q-06, answered):

    • People use eHerkenning at onboarding and for management (FR-03).
    • Systems use an organization certificate from a recognized issuer, linked to the OIN or the KvK number (FR-33, FR-34).

    The candidate connection condition has two parts (AV-04):

    • A private consumer runs its own FSC peer with a PKIoverheid certificate.
    • The private consumer uses the OIN as its legal identity.

    FR-33 allows an OIN or a KvK number. The link between OIN, KvK, and eIDAS identifiers is an open S04 decision. The trust framework for private parties as a whole is PSA decision B-09 in the decision register.

    An integrator connects under FSC delegation. The consent binds the party that the integrator represents (ADR-0034). The consent does not bind the integrator.

    The PKIoverheid question¶

    The first version of ADR-0023 aimed at one infrastructure, with PKIoverheid as the anchor for all certificates, including EUDI. GO and PSA no longer follow that ambition (Q-07, answered). The roles are separate:

    • PKIoverheid is the Dutch PKI for mTLS, FSC, and organization identity.
    • A PuB-EAA carries a qualified electronic seal, from a certificate that a QTSP issues. Neither PKIoverheid nor an access CA seals it (F-66).
    • An access CA issues access certificates only (ETSI TS 119 411-8). An access certificate authenticates an issuer or relying party to the wallet. It never signs attestations.

    Dutch EUDI governance decides which party operates the Dutch access CA. This is a dependency outside the decision scope of GBO.

    EUDI issuance: a certificate set per source¶

    The issuance facility keeps a certificate set per bronhouder. The set has issuer, reader (access), and status-list certificates. The facility never signs with the set of another bronhouder (REQ-58). The issuer name and the purpose that the wallet shows are bound to these certificates. A fallback to the set of another source made the wallet show the wrong issuer (F-50).

    A missing or incomplete set blocks only that source. For the failure cases, see Source onboarding.

    EUDI trust roles¶

    GBO issues attestations for bronhouders. Each of these bronhouders needs its own certificate per role, and one certificate never fills two roles (ADR-0023, part B, proposed, F-81). The roles are as follows:

    Role Purpose Issued by Trust anchor
    Organization or system certificate mTLS for FSC, and the OIN and name PKIoverheid PKIoverheid root
    Access certificate Authenticates the issuer to the wallet (OpenID4VCI) An access CA (ETSI TS 119 411-8) The access CA that the member state notifies
    Registration certificate Proves the registration, per registered service (since Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/1730) A provider that the member state appoints Published by the member state
    Qualified certificate for electronic seal Seals the PuB-EAA. Profile ETSI TS 119 412-6 clause 8. Names the authentic source and the legal basis. Key in a QSCD A QTSP Open (Q-30)
    Status-list certificate Signs the status list for revocation Open (Q-17) Open

    ADR-0028 sets the roles and responsibilities around these certificates:

    • Each bronhouder is the PuB-EAA provider of its attestations. GBO operates the technical issuance facility. GBO is probably the EEAIssuer in the notification of each bronhouder. It is open who designates the Dutch PuB-EAA issuer, and who supervises (Q-28).
    • The issuer registers as relying party with the entitlement PUB_EAA_Provider.
    • It is open on which trust list PuB-EAA providers appear. The options are a List of Trusted Entities (ETSI TS 119 602) or the trusted lists (ETSI TS 119 612). The specifications contradict each other (Q-30). No decision exists for either list.
    • It is open who holds the seal and status-list keys. The options are the central facility, a remote signing service, or an HSM at the bronhouder (Q-17). In the demo, the central issuance server holds them.

    To verify the PID that the wallet discloses, the issuance server also trusts PID issuers. Its list of PID trust anchors holds these CAs (issuance_server.toml.example#L3-L9, issuance_release.go#L120-L121):

    • The BZK pre-production PID issuer CA.
    • The GBO development issuer CA.
    • The issuer CA of every source.

    That list is too broad. It must hold PID-provider anchors only. The callback from the issuance server to the adapter has no caller authentication (REQ-45, ADR-0018).

    ASI trust roles¶

    In the ASI route, the QTSP is not an FSC participant. The chain on FSC starts behind the ASIP (F-87). Two trust points apply:

    • Admission of a QTSP: only a QTSP with the QEAA_Provider right in the register becomes a client of the ASIP (REQ-88). That register and the certificate profile do not exist in demo v0.9.0 (F-85). It is open which identifier links the access certificate of a QTSP to its register entry (Q-35).
    • The seal on a result: the ASIP seals every verification result (REQ-83). The format is open (Q-32, ADR-0041). The demo seals with a development certificate. This certificate is not a qualified seal certificate.

    ADR-0037 describes the ASI front door.

    Demo PKI and production¶

    The demo generates its own trust anchors. Git ignores the private keys, and the demo generates them again for each checkout.

    The demo and production use different PKI parts:

    Area Demo Production
    FSC group root A self-hosted CA: cfssl with a root and intermediate CA (GBO-DEMO Root CA) (fsc-infra/pki/generate-root-ca.sh) PKIoverheid
    FSC certificate requests The FSC ca-certportal in front of cfssl (fsc-infra/docker-compose.yml#L60-L72) The PKIoverheid issuance process of each organization
    Organization certificates Per organization, a group certificate with serialNumber = OIN, and internal mTLS certificates (bootstrap-bd-mock.sh#L48) PKIoverheid certificates of each organization
    PDP AuthZEN endpoint A self-signed certificate that the Inways pin as CA (generate-pdp-cert.sh) An organization certificate
    EUDI issuer and reader CAs Two pre-provisioned development CAs. The demo never generates them, and onboarding stops without them (Makefile#L268-L272) A QTSP for the seal, an access CA, and a registration certificate provider
    Source leaf certificates The development CA provider mints source-bound issuer, reader, and status-list leafs, under a separate privileged command (Makefile#L326-L336) Issued per bronhouder, per role
    ASIP seal and QTSP client certificates From the same development CAs (Makefile#L276-L283, #L320-L324) A qualified seal certificate, and access certificates of admitted QTSPs
    PID trust anchor The BZK pre-production PID issuer CA The notified PID providers

    In the demo, the development CA fills all EUDI roles at the same time. Production separates the roles. The demo is not tested against these standards (F-81):

    • ETSI TS 119 471.
    • The certificate profile.
    • The checks of ETSI TS 119 472-3.

    Open points¶

    These points about identity and trust are open:

    • Can policy read the Peer ID of a consumer as its OIN (Q-68)?
    • The trust list for PuB-EAA providers (Q-30).
    • Custody of the seal and status-list keys (Q-17).
    • Designation of the Dutch PuB-EAA issuer, and supervision (Q-28).
    • The identifier between a QTSP access certificate and its register entry (Q-35).
    • The time limit for a revocation to take effect (Q-53).
    • The trust framework for private parties (PSA B-09).
    • Migration from the self-generated demo CAs.
