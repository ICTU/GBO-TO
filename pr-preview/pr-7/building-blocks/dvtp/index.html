    DvTP channel¶

    In the DvTP channel, a private dienstverlener gets source data about a citizen, with the consent of that citizen. The channel adds two things to GBO Basis. In the toestemmingsvoorziening, the citizen gives and revokes consent. The pseudonimiseervoorziening of BSNk makes sure that the dienstverlener never gets a BSN. This is interaction pattern C of the Globaal Ontwerp.

    The channel has these parts:

    Part Role Zoom in
    Toestemmingsvoorziening The citizen sees the request, logs in, and gives or refuses consent. Later the citizen views and revokes consents. The register signs a consent proof and answers the status of a consent. Toestemmingsvoorziening
    Pseudonimiseervoorziening BSNk PP turns the BSN into values that only one party can read. The channel calls it only when a citizen gives consent. Pseudonymization
    Dienstverlener Starts the request, keeps the proof, and sends it with every query. It never gets a BSN or another number for the citizen. DvTP query
    Integrator A software party that calls the source for a dienstverlener, under an FSC delegation and a registered mandate. ADR-0034
    GBO Basis Checks the proof and the consent status for every request, and fills in the citizen only after an allow. GBO Basis

    Two phases¶

    The channel works in two phases, as in the Globaal Ontwerp. In phase 1, the citizen gives consent. In phase 2, the dienstverlener queries a source under that consent, as often as the consent allows:

    For every hop, with the data and the error paths, see DvTP consent and DvTP query. Viewing and revoking a consent is a third flow: DvTP citizen access.

    Who sees which identifier¶

    The channel is built so that the BSN appears in as few places as possible. The dienstverlener holds no identifier for the citizen at all:

    The consent proof carries one encrypted value per source. Only that source can decrypt its value. The value becomes the own pseudonym of the source, or the BSN if the source has the right to process the BSN (ADR-0032, ADR-0033). In its query, the dienstverlener names the citizen with a fixed placeholder.

    This has a cost. The dienstverlener cannot recognize the same citizen across two consents, and it logs under the consent, not under the person (F-76).

    Compared with the Globaal Ontwerp¶

    The demo follows pattern C of the Globaal Ontwerp. It differs in the form of three steps:

    Step Globaal Ontwerp Demo v0.9.0
    What the dienstverlener gets A consent ID and an encrypted identity A signed consent proof that holds the consent ID and an encrypted value per source
    What the register stores The encrypted identity, scope, purpose, validity The consent, plus the polymorphic values of the citizen under its own reference. No encrypted values per source
    Who checks the consent The bronhouder asks the register The PDP of the source checks the proof itself, then asks the register for the status over FSC

    The design choice behind these differences is in ADR-0032. Whether the register and the portal backend are one system, and which of them talks to BSNk, is open (Q-49, ADR-0040).

    Open points¶

    The main open points in the DvTP channel are these:

    • Whether a private dienstverlener without a public task can connect to BSNk PP at all (Q-01).
    • Who sets the validity period of a consent (Q-13).
    • Whether the proof must also name the integrator (Q-46).
    • The values in the proof are fixed at consent: a source that joins later needs a new consent (F-78).
